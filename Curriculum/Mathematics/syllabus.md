# Mathematics Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `MAT-B01` Numerals, place value, arithmetic, and estimation | `FND-B02` | 1. Base-ten notation and magnitude; 2. Addition/subtraction meanings and algorithms; 3. Multiplication/division models; 4. Order of operations; 5. Mental math, rounding, and bounds | Compute accurately; explain algorithms with place value; estimate and check reasonableness | An equals sign states equality, not “write the answer next” | ☐ Solve mixed arithmetic without calculator; ☐ Explain and estimate each result |
| `MAT-B02` Integers, divisibility, primes, and modular arithmetic | `MAT-B01` | 1. Signed quantities and number line; 2. Integer operations; 3. Factors, multiples, gcd, lcm; 4. Primes and factorization; 5. Remainders and congruence | Operate with integers; prove elementary divisibility claims; solve clock/remainder problems | A negative sign is not always subtraction, and 1 is not prime | ☐ Factor and compute gcd/lcm; ☐ Solve a congruence word problem |
| `MAT-B03` Fractions, ratios, proportions, percentages, and units | `MAT-B01`, `FND-B07` | 1. Fractions as number, quotient, ratio, and operator; 2. Equivalent fractions and operations; 3. Ratios and proportionality; 4. Percent and percent change; 5. Dimensional quantities and conversion | Calculate and interpret rational quantities; distinguish additive from proportional change; track units | Larger denominators do not mean larger fractions, and percent change depends on baseline | ☐ Solve multistep proportion and unit problems; ☐ Explain with diagrams and equations |
| `MAT-B04` Variables, expressions, identities, and algebraic manipulation | `MAT-B02`, `MAT-B03` | 1. Variables and parameters; 2. Expressions and substitution; 3. Properties and equivalent forms; 4. Exponents, radicals, and scientific notation; 5. Factoring and rational expressions | Translate language into expressions; manipulate while preserving equivalence; state domain restrictions | A variable is not merely an unknown, and cancellation applies to factors rather than terms | ☐ Simplify and factor mixed expressions; ☐ Identify illegal transformations |
| `MAT-B05` Equations, inequalities, systems, and coordinate graphs | `MAT-B04` | 1. Solution sets and inverse operations; 2. Linear/quadratic equations; 3. Inequalities and absolute value; 4. Systems and elimination; 5. Coordinates, slope, intercept, and graphs | Solve and verify equations/systems; graph solution sets; connect algebraic and geometric representations | Applying an operation to only one side changes the equation; inequality reverses only under negative multiplication/division | ☐ Solve mixed equations and systems; ☐ Model a constraint graphically |
| `MAT-B06` Functions and major families | `MAT-B05` | 1. Input/output, domain, range, graph, and table; 2. Composition and inverse; 3. Linear and polynomial behavior; 4. Rational, exponential, and logarithmic behavior; 5. Transformations and modeling | Determine and represent functions; compose/invert with restrictions; choose a family from behavior | Every relation is not a function, and an inverse function differs from reciprocal | ☐ Analyze functions across four representations; ☐ Fit and justify a family |
| `MAT-B07` Euclidean geometry, measurement, congruence, similarity, and coordinates | `MAT-B03` | 1. Points, lines, angles, polygons, circles; 2. Perimeter, area, surface, volume; 3. Congruence and rigid motion; 4. Similarity and scale; 5. Coordinate proofs and constructions | Derive standard measures; prove congruence/similarity; solve scale problems | Diagrams are not necessarily to scale, and equal area need not imply congruence | ☐ Complete proof-and-measure problems; ☐ Construct a figure meeting constraints |
| `MAT-B08` Sets, relations, quantifiers, notation, and elementary combinatorics | `FND-I03`, `MAT-B02` | 1. Sets, membership, subset, operations; 2. Product, relation, function, equivalence, order; 3. Quantified statements and negation; 4. Sum/product rules, permutations, combinations; 5. Inclusion-exclusion | Read and create formal notation; negate quantified claims; count without duplication | “Or” in set union is inclusive, and combinations ignore order while permutations do not | ☐ Translate and negate set statements; ☐ Solve counting problems by explicit principle |
| `MAT-I01` Discrete mathematics | `MAT-B08`, `LOG-I01` | 1. Recurrence and recursion; 2. Graphs, walks, paths, cycles, trees; 3. Connectivity, coloring, matching; 4. Invariants and pigeonhole principle; 5. Boolean and finite structures | Model finite problems; prove graph properties; solve recurrence and invariant problems | A graph need not plot a function, and recursive definitions require base cases | ☐ Solve representative graph/counting proofs; ☐ Build a correct discrete model |
| `MAT-I02` Trigonometry, circles, vectors, and analytic geometry | `MAT-B06`, `MAT-B07` | 1. Radians and unit circle; 2. Sine/cosine/tangent graphs and identities; 3. Triangle laws; 4. Vectors, dot product, projections; 5. Lines, conics, and parametric curves | Solve triangles and periodic models; manipulate identities; use vectors geometrically | Trigonometric functions are ratios/coordinates, not angle labels; degrees cannot enter calculus formulas silently | ☐ Prove identities and solve equations; ☐ Model a vector/periodic situation |
| `MAT-I08` Proof methods | `MAT-B08`, `LOG-I02` | 1. Definitions and theorem anatomy; 2. Direct and contrapositive proof; 3. Contradiction and counterexample; 4. Induction and strong induction; 5. Existence, uniqueness, and construction | Write rigorous proofs; choose a method; locate gaps; disprove universals | Examples cannot prove a universal claim, and contradiction requires negating the target correctly | ☐ Write proofs using all core methods; ☐ Critique and repair flawed proofs |
| `MAT-I03` Limits, continuity, derivatives, and differential calculus | `MAT-B06`, `MAT-I02` | 1. Rates and limiting behavior; 2. Epsilon-delta orientation and continuity; 3. Derivative definitions/rules; 4. Implicit and related rates; 5. Linearization, extrema, curve analysis, optimization | Compute and interpret derivatives; justify continuity; solve rate/optimization problems | A limit need not equal the function value, and derivative is a function/rate rather than a symbolic fraction | ☐ Derive rules from definitions; ☐ Analyze and optimize an unfamiliar function |
| `MAT-I04` Integration, fundamental theorem, sequences, and series | `MAT-I03`, `MAT-I08` | 1. Accumulation and Riemann sums; 2. Antiderivatives and fundamental theorem; 3. Substitution, parts, partial fractions; 4. Improper integrals; 5. Sequences, convergence, power/Taylor series | Compute and interpret integrals; connect accumulation and rate; test series convergence | Integral is not merely area or antiderivative notation, and terms approaching zero do not guarantee series convergence | ☐ Solve conceptual and computational integrals; ☐ justify convergence choices |
| `MAT-I05` Multivariable and vector calculus | `MAT-I04`, `MAT-I02` | 1. Multivariable limits and partial derivatives; 2. Gradient and constrained extrema; 3. Multiple integrals and coordinate changes; 4. Vector fields, divergence, curl; 5. Line/surface integrals and Green–Stokes–Gauss | Analyze scalar/vector fields; optimize with constraints; use integral theorems | Partial derivatives do not alone guarantee differentiability, and zero curl has domain conditions | ☐ Solve multivariable optimization/integration; ☐ derive one integral theorem application |
| `MAT-I06` Linear algebra | `MAT-B05`, `MAT-I08` | 1. Linear systems and elimination; 2. Vectors, subspaces, basis, dimension; 3. Linear maps and matrices; 4. Orthogonality, least squares, QR; 5. Determinants, eigenvalues, diagonalization, SVD | Solve systems structurally; prove subspace/map claims; compute and interpret decompositions | Matrices represent linear maps after bases are chosen; determinant is not a generic multiplication device | ☐ Complete proof/computation exam; ☐ Model data or dynamics with eigen/SVD methods |
| `MAT-I07` Ordinary differential equations and dynamical systems | `MAT-I04`, `MAT-I06` | 1. Direction fields and initial-value problems; 2. First-order separable/linear equations; 3. Higher-order linear systems; 4. Laplace and series methods; 5. Phase plane, stability, nonlinear models, and numerics | Solve standard ODEs; interpret parameters and initial conditions; analyze equilibria/stability | A differential equation's solution is a function family, and numerical output needs stability/error analysis | ☐ Solve and verify analytic IVPs; ☐ analyze a nonlinear phase portrait |
| `MAT-A04` Numerical analysis and scientific computing | `MAT-I04`, `MAT-I06`, `CSC-B03` | 1. Floating point, conditioning, stability, error; 2. Root finding and interpolation; 3. Numerical differentiation/integration; 4. Linear systems/eigenproblems; 5. ODE/PDE discretization and reproducibility | Select stable algorithms; bound and measure error; implement reproducible numerical experiments | More digits or smaller steps do not always improve accuracy | ☐ Implement and validate core methods; ☐ distinguish conditioning from algorithmic stability |
| `MAT-A05` Optimization | `MAT-I05`, `MAT-I06` | 1. Unconstrained first/second order conditions; 2. Convex sets/functions; 3. Equality/inequality constraints and KKT; 4. Duality and sensitivity; 5. Gradient, Newton, proximal, stochastic, and variational methods | Formulate and solve optimization problems; prove convexity; interpret dual variables | A local optimum need not be global outside convex settings, and solver convergence does not validate the model | ☐ Derive KKT and dual for a model; ☐ implement and diagnose a solver |
| `MAT-A01` Real analysis and metric spaces | `MAT-I04`, `MAT-I08` | 1. Real-number completeness; 2. Sequences, limits, compactness; 3. Continuity and uniform continuity; 4. Differentiation and Riemann integration; 5. Function sequences and metric spaces | Prove calculus rigorously; construct counterexamples; use compactness/completeness | Pointwise and uniform convergence differ, and bounded sequences need not converge | ☐ Prove core theorems from definitions; ☐ curate counterexamples to false converses |
| `MAT-A09` Measure, integration, and functional analysis | `MAT-A01`, `MAT-I06` | 1. Sigma-algebras and measures; 2. Measurable functions and Lebesgue integral; 3. Convergence theorems; 4. Lp, Banach, and Hilbert spaces; 5. Operators, duality, and spectral orientation | Construct measures/integrals; apply convergence theorems; reason in normed spaces | Almost-everywhere equality is not pointwise equality, and infinite-dimensional intuition differs from finite matrices | ☐ Prove measure/integration results; ☐ solve a Hilbert-space projection problem |
| `MAT-A02` Abstract algebra | `MAT-I01`, `MAT-I08` | 1. Groups and homomorphisms; 2. Quotients and actions; 3. Rings, ideals, and polynomial rings; 4. Fields and extensions; 5. Modules and structure | Prove structural results; construct quotient objects; use isomorphism theorems | Algebraic operation symbols need not mean ordinary addition/multiplication | ☐ Complete group/ring/field proofs; ☐ classify examples by invariant |
| `MAT-A03` Number theory and algebraic structures | `MAT-A02` | 1. Diophantine equations and congruences; 2. Arithmetic functions and quadratic residues; 3. Algebraic integers and finite fields; 4. Analytic number-theory orientation; 5. Cryptographic applications | Prove divisibility/congruence theorems; solve representative Diophantine problems; connect structures to applications | Checking many integers is not proof, and cryptographic hardness differs from impossibility | ☐ Prove major elementary results; ☐ implement one number-theoretic algorithm |
| `MAT-A06` Complex analysis and transforms | `MAT-A01`, `MAT-I05` | 1. Complex differentiation and analyticity; 2. Cauchy theorem/formula; 3. Series, residues, and contour integration; 4. Conformal maps; 5. Fourier/Laplace transform orientation | Use analytic structure and residues; map domains conformally; connect transforms to differential equations | Complex differentiability is far stronger than two-variable differentiability | ☐ Prove/use Cauchy results; ☐ evaluate real integrals by residues |
| `MAT-A07` Topology and differential geometry | `MAT-A01`, `MAT-I08` | 1. Topological spaces, bases, continuity; 2. Connectedness and compactness; 3. Quotients, products, separation; 4. Fundamental group and homology orientation; 5. Manifolds, tangent spaces, forms, curvature | Prove topological properties; distinguish invariant from embedding; compute elementary manifold structures | Continuous deformation preserves topology, not metric shape; compactness depends on the space | ☐ Solve point-set topology proofs; ☐ compute a basic topological/geometric invariant |
| `MAT-A08` Foundations | `MAT-I01`, `MAT-I08`, `LOG-I03` | 1. Axiomatic sets, ordinals, cardinals; 2. Choice and equivalent principles; 3. Formal theories and models; 4. Computability and incompleteness; 5. Alternative foundations orientation | Work with transfinite definitions; distinguish formal truth/provability; state foundational limits | Set-theoretic membership is not subset, and incompleteness does not invalidate mathematics | ☐ Prove basic ordinal/cardinal results; ☐ explain a metamathematical theorem precisely |
| `MAT-A10` Partial differential equations and mathematical physics | `MAT-I05`, `MAT-I07`, `MAT-A01` | 1. Classification and well-posedness; 2. Heat, wave, Laplace equations; 3. Separation, transforms, and Green functions; 4. Characteristics and conservation laws; 5. Weak solutions and numerics | Derive canonical PDEs; solve standard boundary/initial problems; discuss existence, uniqueness, stability | Boundary conditions are part of the problem, and classical solutions may fail to exist | ☐ Solve each canonical PDE; ☐ formulate and analyze a physical boundary-value problem |
| `MAT-E01` Category theory | `MAT-A02`, `MAT-A07`, `MAT-A08` | 1. Categories, functors, natural transformations; 2. Universal properties and adjunctions; 3. Limits/colimits; 4. Monads and algebraic theories; 5. Yoneda and categorical structures across fields | Prove universal properties; construct functors/natural transformations; translate examples between domains | Category theory does not replace element-level proof and abstraction is useful only with faithful examples | ☐ Prove Yoneda and an adjunction result; ☐ model one cross-domain structure categorically |
| `MAT-E02` Research mathematics and theorem creation | `MAT-A01`, `MAT-A02`, `MAT-A07` | 1. Problem selection and literature; 2. Examples, computation, conjecture; 3. Lemma design and proof architecture; 4. Counterexamples and peer criticism; 5. Exposition and publication | Formulate tractable original questions; produce rigorous new arguments; communicate and revise research | Research is not linear theorem proving, and failed conjectures can yield valuable structure | ☐ Complete an original supervised paper; ☐ publicly defend definitions, proofs, and novelty |
