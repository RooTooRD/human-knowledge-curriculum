# Writing Syllabus

## `WRT-B01` — Purpose, audience, genre, and writing process

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B05`, `FND-B06`  
**Lessons:** 1. Writing situations and constraints; 2. Intended reader, prior knowledge, stakes, and action; 3. Genre expectations and models; 4. Discover, plan, draft, revise, edit, publish.  
**Learning outcomes:** Analyze a writing situation; choose a fitting genre and level of detail; create a process plan with separate drafting and revision stages.  
**Common misconceptions:** Good writers rarely produce final-quality first drafts, and “general audience” is not a sufficiently specific audience analysis.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can adapt one message for three audiences and genres. - [ ] Can explain every adaptation by purpose and reader need.

## `WRT-B02` — Sentence boundaries, grammar, punctuation, and usage

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-B01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Clause, phrase, subject, predicate, and modification; 2. Fragments, run-ons, agreement, tense, and reference; 3. Coordination, subordination, and sentence variety; 4. Comma, semicolon, colon, dash, apostrophe, quotation, and capitalization; 5. Usage and dialect/register.  
**Learning outcomes:** Diagnose and repair structural sentence errors; punctuate to reveal syntax and meaning; edit without treating legitimate language variation as incompetence.  
**Common misconceptions:** Punctuation is not merely where speech pauses, and grammatical acceptability is distinct from formal-register convention.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can edit a page and name the rule behind each change. - [ ] Can vary sentence structure without creating ambiguity.

## `WRT-B03` — Paragraph unity, cohesion, emphasis, and transitions

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-B02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Controlling idea and paragraph function; 2. Given/new information, lexical chains, and pronoun reference; 3. Topic position, stress position, and emphasis; 4. Logical and rhetorical transitions; 5. Paragraph length and division.  
**Learning outcomes:** Build a paragraph around one clear function; order sentences for cumulative understanding; revise mechanical transitions into real conceptual links.  
**Common misconceptions:** A paragraph is not defined by a fixed sentence count, and adding “however” cannot repair an absent contrast.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can reorder scrambled sentences and justify the cohesion. - [ ] Can revise a diffuse paragraph into a purposeful one.

## `WRT-B04` — Summary, paraphrase, quotation, and attribution

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-B03`, `FND-I01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Main idea, selection, compression, and neutral representation; 2. Paraphrase by concept rather than synonym substitution; 3. Quotation selection, integration, and analysis; 4. Signal phrases, attribution, and source boundaries; 5. Patchwriting and accidental plagiarism.  
**Learning outcomes:** Write faithful summaries at different lengths; paraphrase without preserving source syntax; integrate quotations with context and interpretation.  
**Common misconceptions:** Changing several words is not adequate paraphrase, and a citation does not excuse copying protected expression without quotation.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can produce summary, paraphrase, and quote versions of one passage. - [ ] Can identify precisely where source material begins and ends.

## `WRT-B05` — Description, narration, explanation, and instruction

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-B03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Concrete observation and selective detail; 2. Event, scene, chronology, pacing, and causation; 3. Definition, example, comparison, mechanism, and causal explanation; 4. User goal, prerequisites, ordered steps, branches, warnings, and verification.  
**Learning outcomes:** Select a mode suited to purpose; explain a process at the audience's level; write testable instructions with error recovery.  
**Common misconceptions:** More detail does not guarantee clarity, and chronological sequence alone may not explain why something happened.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can explain one process to novice and expert readers. - [ ] Can user-test instructions and revise every observed failure.

## `WRT-I01` — Thesis, reasons, evidence, warrants, and objections

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-B04`, `LOG-B03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Arguable, qualified, significant thesis; 2. Reasons and line of reasoning; 3. Evidence quality and relevance; 4. Warrants and assumptions; 5. Objections, concessions, replies, and scope.  
**Learning outcomes:** Construct a defensible argument chain; match evidence to claims; articulate hidden warrants; answer a strong objection without evasion.  
**Common misconceptions:** A thesis is not a topic announcement, and adding quotations does not establish the inference from evidence to claim.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can map every paragraph to a role in the central argument. - [ ] Can write and answer the strongest plausible objection.

## `WRT-I02` — Essay and report architecture

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-B05`, `WRT-I01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Question, scope, and controlling purpose; 2. Classical, problem-solution, comparative, chronological, and IMRaD structures; 3. Sections, headings, previews, recaps, and signposting; 4. Introduction and conclusion functions; 5. Reverse outlining.  
**Learning outcomes:** Choose and justify a document architecture; sequence sections by reader need and argument dependency; diagnose structural failure with a reverse outline.  
**Common misconceptions:** A five-paragraph form is not a universal essay structure, and introductions need not be written first.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can produce two viable outlines and explain the trade-off. - [ ] Can restructure a draft without merely editing sentences.

## `WRT-I03` — Revision at argument, structure, paragraph, sentence, and word levels

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-I02`, `LRN-B06`  
**Lessons:** 1. Revision hierarchy and diagnostic reading; 2. Argument and evidence revision; 3. Section and paragraph revision; 4. Style and line editing; 5. Proofreading, version comparison, and feedback triage.  
**Learning outcomes:** Revise from global to local concerns; separate substantive revision from proofreading; evaluate feedback against purpose rather than obeying mechanically.  
**Common misconceptions:** Revision is not cosmetic correction, and preserving drafted text is not a virtue when it no longer serves the argument.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can submit before/after drafts with a substantive change log. - [ ] Can justify accepted and rejected feedback.

## `WRT-I04` — Style: clarity, precision, concision, voice, rhythm, and register

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-B03`, `WRT-I03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Concrete subjects and strong verbs; 2. Nominalization, abstraction, passive voice, and agency; 3. Redundancy, qualification, and exact word choice; 4. Sentence rhythm, emphasis, parallelism, and variation; 5. Voice and register by community and genre.  
**Learning outcomes:** Edit opaque prose while preserving nuance; use passive voice intentionally; vary rhythm and register without sacrificing precision.  
**Common misconceptions:** Concision does not mean shortest possible, and passive voice is a tool rather than a universal error.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can cut a draft by 20% without losing content. - [ ] Can explain the rhetorical effect of major stylistic choices.

## `WRT-I05` — Citation systems, source synthesis, and plagiarism avoidance

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-B04`, `RSH-B02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Why and when to cite; 2. Author-date, notes-bibliography, numeric, and legal systems; 3. Bibliographic elements and reference tools; 4. Synthesis matrices and multi-source paragraphs; 5. Plagiarism, self-plagiarism, common knowledge, and permissions.  
**Learning outcomes:** Apply one citation system consistently; synthesize sources by issue rather than one-at-a-time summary; identify citation, quotation, and permission obligations.  
**Common misconceptions:** A bibliography without in-text attribution is insufficient, and citation does not cure copyright infringement or fabricated evidence.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can produce an error-free referenced passage from mixed source types. - [ ] Can trace each noncommon claim to supporting evidence.

## `WRT-A01` — Academic article, literature review, and research proposal

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-I02`, `WRT-I05`, `RSH-I02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Scholarly conversation, contribution, and disciplinary genre; 2. Abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and limitations; 3. Narrative, scoping, and systematic review logic; 4. Proposal problem, aims, methods, feasibility, ethics, and significance; 5. Submission and revision.  
**Learning outcomes:** Draft a coherent scholarly manuscript; synthesize a literature into claims and gaps; write a feasible proposal whose methods answer its question.  
**Common misconceptions:** A literature review is not an annotated bibliography, and novelty without significance or feasibility is not a contribution.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can produce a review with an explicit synthesis architecture. - [ ] Can defend alignment among question, evidence, method, and claim.

## `WRT-A02` — Technical, scientific, legal, policy, and business writing

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-I04`, `WRT-I05`  
**Lessons:** 1. Requirements, specifications, procedures, and technical reports; 2. Scientific uncertainty, figures, methods, and results; 3. Legal issue-rule-analysis-conclusion and authority; 4. Policy brief problem-options-criteria-recommendation; 5. Executive summary, memo, proposal, and decision record.  
**Learning outcomes:** Produce documents that enable action and audit; state uncertainty and constraints precisely; adapt evidence and organization to professional conventions.  
**Common misconceptions:** Jargon is not evidence of technical competence, and executive summaries are not introductions but stand-alone decision aids.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can translate one analysis into technical, policy, and executive formats. - [ ] Can pass a user test for accuracy and actionable clarity.

## `WRT-A03` — Public scholarship, journalism, editing, and publication

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-I03`, `COM-I04`, `RSH-I05`  
**Lessons:** 1. Public question, news value, angle, and reader service; 2. Reporting, interviewing, verification, attribution, and correction; 3. Headline, lead, nut graph, feature, explainer, and op-ed; 4. Developmental, substantive, copy, and proof editing; 5. Pitching, publishing, audience feedback, and ethics.  
**Learning outcomes:** Translate expertise without distortion; report and verify a public-facing piece; edit for argument, accuracy, accessibility, and style.  
**Common misconceptions:** Simplification need not mean falsification, and neutrality does not require presenting unsupported claims as equivalent.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can publish an evidence-linked explainer for nonspecialists. - [ ] Can document fact-checks, edits, conflicts, and corrections.

## `WRT-A04` — Creative nonfiction, fiction, poetry, and dramatic writing

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-I04`, `LIT-I03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Image, scene, voice, character, desire, and conflict; 2. Narrative distance, point of view, time, and structure; 3. Poetic line, sound, form, figurative language, and compression; 4. Dialogue, action, stage/screen logic; 5. Workshop, imitation, revision, and ethical representation.  
**Learning outcomes:** Create sustained work using intentional formal choices; revise by diagnosing reader experience; distinguish invention from ethical claims about real persons.  
**Common misconceptions:** Creative freedom does not eliminate craft constraints or responsibility, and obscurity is not automatically depth.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can complete and substantially revise work in two genres. - [ ] Can give and use craft-specific workshop feedback.

## `WRT-E01` — Sustained original scholarly or literary work

**Prerequisites:** `WRT-A01`, `WRT-A03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Project conception, contribution, audience, and scope; 2. Long-form architecture and research system; 3. Production schedule, chapter/workshop cycle, and version control; 4. Editorial collaboration, permissions, and publication strategy; 5. Coherence, final revision, and public defense.  
**Learning outcomes:** Conceive, manage, complete, and defend a book-length or equivalent project; maintain evidential and stylistic coherence over long form; revise through multiple external reviews.  
**Common misconceptions:** Sustained work is not merely accumulated short pieces, and persistence cannot substitute for rethinking a flawed central design.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can deliver a complete, externally reviewed manuscript or equivalent. - [ ] Can articulate its contribution, limitations, audience, and revision history.
