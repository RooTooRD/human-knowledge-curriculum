# Writing Glossary

These are curriculum-scoped working definitions. They identify how a term is used
in its unit and the operation mastery requires; consult the unit’s advanced
reference for competing, historical, or specialist definitions.

| Unit | Terms |
|---|---|
| `WRT-B01` | **Purpose:** Within Purpose, audience, genre, and writing process, the organizing beginner idea that connects writing situations and constraints with intended reader, prior knowledge, stakes, and action; in this curriculum it supports the ability to analyze a writing situation.<br>**audience:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through discover, plan, draft, revise, edit, publish; mastery includes the ability to choose a fitting genre and level of detail. |
| `WRT-B02` | **Sentence boundaries:** Within Sentence boundaries, grammar, punctuation, and usage, the organizing beginner idea that connects clause, phrase, subject, predicate, and modification with fragments, run-ons, agreement, tense, and reference; in this curriculum it supports the ability to diagnose and repair structural sentence errors.<br>**grammar:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through usage and dialect/register; mastery includes the ability to punctuate to reveal syntax and meaning. |
| `WRT-B03` | **Paragraph unity:** Within Paragraph unity, cohesion, emphasis, and transitions, the organizing beginner idea that connects controlling idea and paragraph function with given/new information, lexical chains, and pronoun reference; in this curriculum it supports the ability to build a paragraph around one clear function.<br>**cohesion:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through paragraph length and division; mastery includes the ability to order sentences for cumulative understanding. |
| `WRT-B04` | **Summary:** Within Summary, paraphrase, quotation, and attribution, the organizing beginner idea that connects main idea, selection, compression, and neutral representation with paraphrase by concept rather than synonym substitution; in this curriculum it supports the ability to write faithful summaries at different lengths.<br>**paraphrase:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through patchwriting and accidental plagiarism; mastery includes the ability to paraphrase without preserving source syntax. |
| `WRT-B05` | **Description:** Within Description, narration, explanation, and instruction, the organizing beginner idea that connects concrete observation and selective detail with event, scene, chronology, pacing, and causation; in this curriculum it supports the ability to select a mode suited to purpose.<br>**narration:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through user goal, prerequisites, ordered steps, branches, warnings, and verification; mastery includes the ability to explain a process at the audience's level. |
| `WRT-I01` | **Thesis:** Within Thesis, reasons, evidence, warrants, and objections, the organizing intermediate idea that connects arguable, qualified, significant thesis with reasons and line of reasoning; in this curriculum it supports the ability to construct a defensible argument chain.<br>**reasons:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through objections, concessions, replies, and scope; mastery includes the ability to match evidence to claims. |
| `WRT-I02` | **Essay architecture:** Within Essay and report architecture, the organizing intermediate idea that connects question, scope, and controlling purpose with classical, problem-solution, comparative, chronological, and IMRaD structures; in this curriculum it supports the ability to choose and justify a document architecture.<br>**report architecture:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through reverse outlining; mastery includes the ability to sequence sections by reader need and argument dependency. |
| `WRT-I03` | **Revision at argument:** Within Revision at argument, structure, paragraph, sentence, and word levels, the organizing intermediate idea that connects revision hierarchy and diagnostic reading with argument and evidence revision; in this curriculum it supports the ability to revise from global to local concerns.<br>**structure:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through proofreading, version comparison, and feedback triage; mastery includes the ability to separate substantive revision from proofreading. |
| `WRT-I04` | **Style:** Within Style: clarity, precision, concision, voice, rhythm, and register, the organizing intermediate idea that connects concrete subjects and strong verbs with nominalization, abstraction, passive voice, and agency; in this curriculum it supports the ability to edit opaque prose while preserving nuance.<br>**clarity:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through voice and register by community and genre; mastery includes the ability to use passive voice intentionally. |
| `WRT-I05` | **Citation systems:** Within Citation systems, source synthesis, and plagiarism avoidance, the organizing intermediate idea that connects why and when to cite with author-date, notes-bibliography, numeric, and legal systems; in this curriculum it supports the ability to apply one citation system consistently.<br>**source synthesis:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through plagiarism, self-plagiarism, common knowledge, and permissions; mastery includes the ability to synthesize sources by issue rather than one-at-a-time summary. |
| `WRT-A01` | **Academic article:** Within Academic article, literature review, and research proposal, the organizing advanced idea that connects scholarly conversation, contribution, and disciplinary genre with abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and limitations; in this curriculum it supports the ability to draft a coherent scholarly manuscript.<br>**literature review:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through submission and revision; mastery includes the ability to synthesize a literature into claims and gaps. |
| `WRT-A02` | **Technical:** Within Technical, scientific, legal, policy, and business writing, the organizing advanced idea that connects requirements, specifications, procedures, and technical reports with scientific uncertainty, figures, methods, and results; in this curriculum it supports the ability to produce documents that enable action and audit.<br>**scientific:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through executive summary, memo, proposal, and decision record; mastery includes the ability to state uncertainty and constraints precisely. |
| `WRT-A03` | **Public scholarship:** Within Public scholarship, journalism, editing, and publication, the organizing advanced idea that connects public question, news value, angle, and reader service with reporting, interviewing, verification, attribution, and correction; in this curriculum it supports the ability to translate expertise without distortion.<br>**journalism:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through pitching, publishing, audience feedback, and ethics; mastery includes the ability to report and verify a public-facing piece. |
| `WRT-A04` | **Creative nonfiction:** Within Creative nonfiction, fiction, poetry, and dramatic writing, the organizing advanced idea that connects image, scene, voice, character, desire, and conflict with narrative distance, point of view, time, and structure; in this curriculum it supports the ability to create sustained work using intentional formal choices.<br>**fiction:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through workshop, imitation, revision, and ethical representation; mastery includes the ability to revise by diagnosing reader experience. |
| `WRT-E01` | **Sustained original scholarly or literary work:** Within Sustained original scholarly or literary work, the organizing expert idea that connects project conception, contribution, audience, and scope with long-form architecture and research system; in this curriculum it supports the ability to conceive, manage, complete, and defend a book-length or equivalent project.<br>**or literary work:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through coherence, final revision, and public defense; mastery includes the ability to maintain evidential and stylistic coherence over long form. |
