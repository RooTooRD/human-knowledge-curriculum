# Astronomy Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AST-B01` Sky, coordinates, time, seasons, phases, eclipses, observation | `FND-B07`, `GEO-B01` | 1. Celestial sphere and daily motion; 2. altitude/azimuth and equatorial coordinates; 3. calendars/seasons; 4. Moon phases/eclipses/tides; 5. observation planning | Predict visible sky events; use coordinates/time; explain seasons/phases geometrically | Seasons are caused mainly by axial tilt, not Earth–Sun distance | ☐ Keep verified sky log; ☐ model phase/eclipse geometry |
| `AST-B02` Telescopes, detectors, spectra, images, and measurement | `AST-B01`, `PHY-B05` | 1. Aperture/resolution; 2. optical/radio/space telescopes; 3. detectors/images; 4. spectra/Doppler; 5. calibration/noise | Choose instruments by wavelength/question; interpret spectra/images; estimate resolution | Magnification without aperture/resolution does not reveal detail | ☐ Analyze calibrated observation; ☐ design observing proposal |
| `AST-B03` Solar system | `AST-B02`, `PHY-B03` | 1. Formation and orbital dynamics; 2. Sun/terrestrial planets; 3. giants/moons; 4. asteroids/comets; 5. exploration/comparative planetology | Explain system architecture; compare planetary evolution; calculate basic orbits | Planets are not equally spaced static spheres | ☐ Construct comparative planetary dossier; ☐ solve orbital problem |
| `AST-I01` Stellar structure, fusion, classification, evolution, and death | `AST-B02`, `PHY-I04`, `CHM-B02` | 1. Luminosity, temperature, spectra, HR diagram; 2. hydrostatic equilibrium/fusion; 3. formation/main sequence; 4. giants/white dwarfs; 5. supernovae/neutron stars/black holes | Infer stellar properties; trace mass-dependent evolution; explain nucleosynthesis | More massive stars live shorter despite having more fuel | ☐ Analyze cluster HR diagram; ☐ derive stellar scaling |
| `AST-I02` Milky Way, interstellar medium, populations, and dynamics | `AST-I01`, `PHY-I01` | 1. Galactic components; 2. gas/dust/star formation; 3. rotation and mass; 4. populations/chemical history; 5. central black hole | Map Milky Way structure; infer mass from motion; connect enrichment to populations | Milky Way images from outside are reconstructions, not photographs | ☐ Analyze rotation/population data; ☐ explain spiral structure evidence |
| `AST-I03` Galaxies, active nuclei, clusters, and structure | `AST-I02`, `PHY-I05` | 1. Morphology/scaling; 2. formation/evolution/mergers; 3. active nuclei/accretion; 4. groups/clusters/lensing; 5. cosmic web | Classify/infer galaxy properties; explain feedback and environment; use lensing/dynamics | Galaxy morphology is not a fixed evolutionary sequence | ☐ Analyze survey sample; ☐ derive cluster/lensing mass estimate |
| `AST-I04` Expanding universe, Big Bang evidence, dark matter, dark energy | `AST-I03`, `PHY-I05`, `STA-I03` | 1. Distance ladder/redshift; 2. expansion and Hubble law; 3. background radiation/nucleosynthesis; 4. dark matter evidence; 5. acceleration/dark energy | Evaluate converging cosmological evidence; calculate simple expansion relations; distinguish components | Expansion is not galaxies flying from a central explosion into empty space | ☐ Build evidence map; ☐ solve distance-redshift problem |
| `AST-I05` Exoplanets, habitability, biosignatures, astrobiology | `AST-B03`, `BIO-I05`, `CHM-I03` | 1. Transit/radial velocity/imaging; 2. planet populations; 3. habitability and climate; 4. life's origin/extremes; 5. biosignatures and false positives | Infer planet properties; assess habitability cautiously; design biosignature tests | Habitable does not mean inhabited, and one gas rarely proves life | ☐ Analyze light/radial-velocity data; ☐ critique biosignature claim |
| `AST-A01` Relativistic astrophysics | `AST-I01`, `PHY-A04` | 1. White dwarfs/neutron stars; 2. black holes; 3. accretion/jets; 4. compact mergers/waves; 5. multi-messenger astronomy | Model compact objects; interpret relativistic observations; combine messengers | Black holes do not indiscriminately vacuum distant matter | ☐ Solve compact-object problem; ☐ analyze multi-messenger event |
| `AST-A02` Cosmology | `AST-I04`, `PHY-A04`, `MAT-A10` | 1. FRW geometry/dynamics; 2. thermal history/nucleosynthesis; 3. inflation; 4. perturbations/background; 5. structure, parameters, tensions | Derive expansion equations; calculate thermal milestones; interpret parameter inference | Big Bang theory describes early evolution, not necessarily absolute origin | ☐ Complete cosmology derivations; ☐ assess parameter tension |
| `AST-A03` Observational astronomy, surveys, inference, computation | `AST-B02`, `STA-A02`, `PHY-I08` | 1. Proposal/selection function; 2. reduction/calibration; 3. catalogs/completeness; 4. time-domain/spatial inference; 5. simulation/archives/reproducibility | Design survey/observation; reduce data; model selection and uncertainty | Telescope time and pipelines impose selection effects before analysis | ☐ Reduce public data end-to-end; ☐ defend selection model |
| `AST-A04` History and philosophy of cosmological worldviews | `AST-I04`, `HST-I07`, `REL-I06` | 1. Ancient/medieval cosmologies; 2. Islamic/European astronomy; 3. heliocentrism/mechanics; 4. relativity/expanding universe; 5. creation, finitude, fine-tuning, limits | Contextualize models; compare scientific/theological questions; avoid conflict myths | Changing cosmology neither automatically proves nor disproves theology | ☐ Analyze primary texts/models; ☐ separate empirical and metaphysical claims |
| `AST-E01` Frontier astrophysics and cosmology research | `AST-A01`, `AST-A02`, `AST-A03` | 1. Frontier question/literature; 2. instrument/survey/simulation; 3. inference/blinding; 4. robustness/systematics; 5. publication/data release | Conduct original astronomical analysis; control systematics; communicate discovery limits | Statistical anomaly is not discovery without systematics and replication | ☐ Produce research-grade analysis; ☐ pass independent reproducibility review |
