# Political Science Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `POL-B01` Power, authority, legitimacy, state, nation, government, citizenship | `FND-I05`, `HST-B03` | 1. Power forms; 2. authority/legitimacy; 3. state/government; 4. nation/identity; 5. citizenship/civil society | Distinguish core concepts; map authority; analyze legitimacy claims | State, government, regime, and nation are not synonyms | ☐ Analyze one polity; ☐ compare legitimacy theories |
| `POL-B02` Regimes, constitutions, democracy, autocracy, representation, accountability | `POL-B01`, `PHI-I06` | 1. Regime types; 2. constitutional limits; 3. democratic participation/competition; 4. authoritarian control; 5. representation/accountability | Classify regimes with evidence; evaluate accountability; compare constitutional designs | Elections alone do not establish liberal democracy | ☐ Build regime evidence profile; ☐ analyze design trade-offs |
| `POL-B03` Political ideologies | `POL-B01`, `HST-I03` | 1. Ideology and left/right limits; 2. liberal/conservative; 3. socialist/anarchist; 4. nationalist/religious; 5. feminist/green/postcolonial | Reconstruct ideologies charitably; compare views of person/state/economy; trace hybrids | Ideologies are contested traditions, not one fixed platform | ☐ Analyze primary texts; ☐ compare positions across common questions |
| `POL-I01` Comparative politics | `POL-B02`, `STA-B02` | 1. Comparative method; 2. executives/legislatures; 3. courts/federalism; 4. electoral/party systems; 5. institutional outcomes | Compare institutions; explain case selection; assess causal claims | One country's institution cannot be transplanted without context | ☐ Comparative case study; ☐ predict reform consequences |
| `POL-I02` Political behavior | `POL-B02`, `PSY-I04`, `COM-I05` | 1. Opinion/identity; 2. participation/turnout; 3. parties/campaigns; 4. media/polarization; 5. movements/collective action | Analyze survey/behavior evidence; explain participation; evaluate persuasion claims | Voters are neither perfectly informed calculators nor uniformly irrational | ☐ Analyze public-opinion data; ☐ design participation study |
| `POL-I03` Political economy, capacity, corruption, development, distribution | `POL-B01`, `ECO-I03` | 1. State/market institutions; 2. taxation/capacity; 3. rent/corruption; 4. redistribution; 5. development coalitions | Diagnose capacity/incentives; compare anti-corruption tools; analyze distribution | Corruption is not only individual morality; institutional incentives matter | ☐ Produce country diagnosis; ☐ design feasible reform |
| `POL-I04` Public policy | `POL-I01`, `RSH-I04`, `ECO-I03` | 1. Problem/agenda; 2. instruments; 3. adoption; 4. implementation; 5. evaluation/feedback/failure | Build policy theory; compare instruments; evaluate implementation | Policy enactment is not policy effect | ☐ Write options brief; ☐ evaluate real policy chain |
| `POL-I05` Public administration | `POL-I04`, `BUS-I02` | 1. Bureaucracy/merit; 2. budgeting; 3. regulation; 4. service delivery; 5. accountability/digital government | Design administrative process; analyze budget/service; balance discretion/control | Bureaucracy is not inherently waste; it can secure consistency and rights | ☐ Redesign service; ☐ audit administrative accountability |
| `POL-I06` International relations | `POL-B01`, `HST-I04`, `GEO-I02` | 1. Sovereignty/system; 2. realism/liberalism/constructivism; 3. diplomacy/war; 4. institutions/law; 5. trade/global governance | Compare theories; analyze conflict/cooperation; trace diplomatic options | States are not unitary rational actors in every context | ☐ Analyze crisis through three theories; ☐ draft diplomatic strategy |
| `POL-A01` Comparative constitutionalism, erosion, coups, revolutions, transitions | `POL-I01`, `HST-I05` | 1. Constitutional origins; 2. courts/executive constraints; 3. erosion; 4. coups/revolutions; 5. transition/consolidation | Identify erosion indicators; compare transition paths; evaluate safeguards | Democratic breakdown is often incremental and legalistic | ☐ Produce early-warning assessment; ☐ compare transition cases |
| `POL-A02` Security studies | `POL-I06`, `SYS-A01` | 1. Strategic theory; 2. deterrence/coercion; 3. alliances/arms; 4. civil war/terrorism; 5. peace/settlement | Link force to political aims; model escalation; assess peace design | Military superiority does not ensure political victory | ☐ Write strategic estimate; ☐ negotiate conflict simulation |
| `POL-A03` Global governance | `POL-I03`, `POL-I06`, `SYS-A05` | 1. International organizations; 2. development/finance; 3. climate/health; 4. technology/migration; 5. legitimacy/compliance | Map fragmented governance; assess effectiveness/justice; propose coordination | Treaties and organizations lack uniform authority/capacity | ☐ Analyze regime complex; ☐ design governance improvement |
| `POL-A04` Political methodology | `POL-I01`, `STA-A01`, `RSH-A01` | 1. Concepts/measurement; 2. cases/comparison; 3. causal designs; 4. formal models; 5. mixed/interpretive evidence | Design rigorous political research; measure contested concepts; integrate evidence | Quantification does not remove political assumptions from measures | ☐ Preregister study; ☐ defend method before rival traditions |
| `POL-A05` Normative institutional analysis and statecraft | `POL-I04`, `PHI-I06`, `LAW-A01` | 1. Legitimate ends; 2. institutional feasibility; 3. rights/trade-offs; 4. prudence/dirty hands; 5. accountability/learning | Combine normative and empirical analysis; make responsible recommendations | Good intentions do not establish legitimate means or likely outcomes | ☐ Write statecraft memorandum; ☐ defend under adversarial ethics review |
| `POL-E01` Political research and policy design | `POL-A03`, `POL-A04`, `RSH-E01` | 1. Original question; 2. theory/evidence; 3. stakeholder/institution; 4. policy design; 5. scholarly/public defense | Produce original political inquiry; translate findings without overclaiming | Policy relevance does not excuse weak identification or partisan evidence selection | ☐ Complete thesis/policy artifact; ☐ pass replication and oral defense |
