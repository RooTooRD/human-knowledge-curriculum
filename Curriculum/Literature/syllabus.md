# Literature Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `LIT-B01` Close reading | `FND-B05`, `WRT-B05` | 1. Speaker/scene; 2. diction/image; 3. symbol/motif; 4. tone/ambiguity; 5. form/evidence | Make precise observations; build interpretation from textual evidence; distinguish narrator/author | An interpretation is not supported merely because it is personally meaningful | ☐ Annotate unseen text; ☐ write evidence-dense close reading |
| `LIT-B02` Narrative | `LIT-B01` | 1. Plot/story; 2. time/pacing; 3. perspective/focalization; 4. character/world; 5. genre/unreliability | Analyze narrative construction; compare perspectives; explain reader effects | Narrator and author are not identical | ☐ Analyze short narrative; ☐ transform viewpoint and explain effects |
| `LIT-B03` Poetry | `LIT-B01`, `LIN-B02` | 1. Line/stanza; 2. meter/rhythm; 3. rhyme/sound; 4. figure/image; 5. forms/free verse/translation | Scan and hear poems; analyze form-meaning relation; compare translations | Free verse is not formless prose broken into lines | ☐ Perform/scan poem; ☐ comparative translation reading |
| `LIT-B04` Drama and performance | `LIT-B01`, `COM-B03` | 1. Action/conflict; 2. dialogue/subtext; 3. scene/structure; 4. stage/audience; 5. performance/adaptation | Read performatively; analyze staging choices; compare text/performance | A play's meaning is not exhausted by silent reading | ☐ Stage/analyze scene; ☐ write adaptation rationale |
| `LIT-I01` Interpretation, evidence, context, intertext, canon, reader | `LIT-B02`, `HST-B02`, `WRT-I01` | 1. Interpretive question; 2. textual warrant; 3. historical context; 4. intertext/reception; 5. canon/reader communities | Build bounded interpretations; use context without reduction; compare receptions | Authorial intent is relevant evidence but not always final authority | ☐ Defend interpretation orally; ☐ revise against rival reading |
| `LIT-I02` Genres and oral traditions | `LIT-B02`, `LIT-B03`, `LIT-B04` | 1. Epic/lyric; 2. drama/tale/romance; 3. novel/short story; 4. essay/memoir; 5. scripture/oral performance | Recognize genre conventions/history; compare oral/written forms; analyze hybrid works | Genres are changing expectations, not rigid boxes | ☐ Genre-comparison portfolio; ☐ analyze hybrid work |
| `LIT-I03` Literary criticism | `LIT-I01`, `PHI-I04`, `SOC-I04` | 1. Formalist; 2. historicist/Marxist; 3. psychoanalytic; 4. feminist/queer; 5. postcolonial/ecocritical/reader approaches | Apply theories accurately; compare what they reveal/omit; avoid jargon substitution | Critical theory should generate textual insight, not predetermined verdict | ☐ Multi-lens analysis; ☐ defend method selection |
| `LIT-I04` Ancient and classical world traditions | `LIT-I02`, `HST-B05` | 1. African/West Asian; 2. South Asian; 3. East Asian; 4. Mediterranean; 5. American/oral traditions | Read works in context; compare forms/themes; trace transmission | “Classical” is not synonymous with Greco-Roman | ☐ Comparative primary-text exam; ☐ contextual performance |
| `LIT-I05` Medieval and early modern world literatures | `LIT-I04`, `HST-I01` | 1. Arabic/Persian; 2. African/oral/manuscript; 3. European; 4. South/East Asian; 5. travel, devotion, court, print | Compare connected traditions; analyze manuscript/oral contexts; trace genre movement | Medieval literature was not globally isolated or universally religious | ☐ Connected-literature essay; ☐ source/transmission map |
| `LIT-I06` Modern world literature | `LIT-I05`, `HST-I05` | 1. Novel/modernity; 2. colonial/anticolonial; 3. realism/modernism; 4. nation/diaspora; 5. decolonization/global circulation | Relate form to historical transformation; compare modernities; analyze translation/canon | Modernism has multiple global histories, not one diffusion from Europe | ☐ Comparative modernity essay; ☐ reception analysis |
| `LIT-I07` Contemporary literature, film, digital narrative, genre fiction | `LIT-I06`, `COM-I05` | 1. Post-1990 fiction/poetry; 2. film language/adaptation; 3. speculative/crime/romance; 4. graphic/game/digital narrative; 5. platforms/global markets | Analyze media-specific form; evaluate genre; trace platform/market influence | Popularity and literary value are neither identical nor mutually exclusive | ☐ Cross-media analysis; ☐ curate contemporary syllabus |
| `LIT-A01` Comparative literature, translation, reception, world-literature debates | `LIT-I03`, `LIN-I08` | 1. Comparison/commensurability; 2. translation theory; 3. circulation/reception; 4. world-system/canon; 5. multilingual reading ethics | Compare without flattening; analyze translation choices; trace circulation/power | World literature is not simply a list of works from many countries | ☐ Comparative translation project; ☐ defend corpus selection |
| `LIT-A02` Textual scholarship, manuscripts, editions, archives, digital humanities | `LIT-I01`, `RSH-I05`, `CSC-B04` | 1. Material text/codicology; 2. variants/stemmatics; 3. editing/annotation; 4. archive/bibliography; 5. corpus/digital edition | Collate variants; build critical edition; use computation transparently | A “definitive” text may erase legitimate versions and performance traditions | ☐ Produce mini critical edition; ☐ document editorial decisions |
| `LIT-A03` Literature, ethics, religion, politics, ecology, science, mind | `LIT-I03`, `PHI-I05` | 1. Literature/moral imagination; 2. sacred/secular; 3. ideology/power; 4. environment/science; 5. cognition/empathy limits | Use literature in interdisciplinary inquiry without treating fiction as data transparently | Reading fiction does not automatically make readers more empathetic or virtuous | ☐ Interdisciplinary essay; ☐ state evidential boundaries |
| `LIT-E01` Advanced literary scholarship or creative-critical work | `LIT-A01`, `LIT-A02`, `RSH-E01` | 1. Original corpus/question; 2. languages/archive; 3. theory/method; 4. sustained argument or creative-critical form; 5. publication/defense | Produce original literary contribution; handle texts/material history rigorously; articulate form | Obscure corpus or theory is not originality by itself | ☐ Complete thesis/manuscript; ☐ pass scholarly/creative defense |
