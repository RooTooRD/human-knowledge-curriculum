# The Human Knowledge Curriculum

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is a lifelong curriculum for an educated person, not a degree program,
> credential, or claim that every field can be mastered equally.

The graph begins at ordinary language, observation, counting, and self-regulation. It
then develops reasoning, quantitative and evidential literacy, disciplinary models, and
finally integrative judgment. A learner may branch, but may not skip prerequisites.

## Governing principles

- **Dependency first:** every unit points to prior units by stable ID.
- **No hidden starting level:** foundational reading, numeracy, digital operation, and
  learning skills are explicit.
- **Core before extension:** `Core` means part of the smallest defensible common
  curriculum; `Extension` preserves advanced depth or a specialist path.
- **Competence, not exposure:** later phases will pair each node with measurable
  outcomes and assessments.
- **Multiple civilizations:** historical and intellectual sequences are global, with
  Islamic civilization studied both as part of world history and on its own terms.
- **Epistemic transparency:** empirical, mathematical, historical, interpretive,
  normative, legal, and theological claims use their appropriate standards of warrant.
- **Maintenance:** fast-changing domains such as AI, security, health, law, and climate
  require dated sources and scheduled revision.

## Difficulty and status

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Can be learned from the explicit zero-prerequisite foundation |
| Intermediate | Requires one or more established methods or disciplinary models |
| Advanced | Requires synthesis, proof, design, or evaluation across several units |
| Expert | Research-level orientation or mature independent judgment |
| Core | Required for the common lifelong curriculum |
| Extension | Optional depth; required only for a chosen specialist route |
