# Biology Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BIO-B01` Life, cells, membranes, biomolecules, microscopy | `CHM-B03`, `RSH-B03` | 1. Life and cell theory; 2. prokaryote/eukaryote; 3. macromolecules; 4. membranes/organelles; 5. microscopy/scale | Identify cell structures; connect molecules to function; interpret micrographs | Cells are dynamic systems, not bags of organelles | ☐ Annotate unknown cells; ☐ design microscopy observation |
| `BIO-B02` Energy, enzymes, respiration, photosynthesis, metabolism | `BIO-B01`, `CHM-B05` | 1. Free energy/coupling; 2. enzymes/regulation; 3. ATP/redox; 4. respiration/fermentation; 5. photosynthesis/metabolic networks | Analyze enzyme data; trace matter/electrons/energy; compare metabolic strategies | Organisms use but do not create energy; plants also respire | ☐ Balance pathway inputs/outputs; ☐ interpret kinetics experiment |
| `BIO-B03` DNA, genes, chromosomes, expression, division | `BIO-B01`, `CHM-I03` | 1. DNA/RNA structure; 2. replication/repair; 3. transcription/translation; 4. regulation; 5. mitosis/meiosis/mutation | Trace information flow; predict mutation effects; compare cell divisions | One gene does not usually map to one fixed trait | ☐ Solve expression/mutation cases; ☐ model meiosis |
| `BIO-I01` Mendelian, molecular, quantitative, population genetics | `BIO-B03`, `STA-B03` | 1. Segregation/assortment/linkage; 2. pedigrees/probability; 3. gene interaction; 4. quantitative traits; 5. allele frequencies/evolutionary forces | Predict crosses/pedigrees; analyze complex traits; calculate population change | Dominant does not mean common, stronger, or beneficial | ☐ Complete genetic analyses; ☐ infer mechanism from data |
| `BIO-B04` Evolution | `BIO-B03`, `STA-B03` | 1. Variation/inheritance; 2. natural/sexual selection; 3. drift/gene flow; 4. adaptation/speciation; 5. evidence/common descent | Explain change mechanistically; distinguish selection/drift; evaluate evidence | Individuals do not evolve because they need to adapt | ☐ Analyze evolving population; ☐ rebut common misconception with evidence |
| `BIO-I02` Phylogeny, systematics, tree of life, history of life | `BIO-B04`, `MAT-I01` | 1. Homology/analogy; 2. cladograms/characters; 3. molecular phylogenetics; 4. domains/diversity; 5. fossils/radiations/extinctions | Read/build trees; infer ancestral states; integrate fossil/molecular evidence | Living species are not ancestors of other living species merely because “primitive” | ☐ Reconstruct phylogeny; ☐ interpret uncertainty |
| `BIO-B05` Organisms, structure-function, homeostasis, development, behavior | `BIO-B01`, `BIO-B02` | 1. Organization/tissues; 2. exchange/transport; 3. regulation/homeostasis; 4. development/life cycle; 5. behavior/ecological function | Relate form to function; model feedback; compare organism strategies | Homeostasis is regulated change, not constancy of every variable | ☐ Analyze organismal system; ☐ predict perturbation response |
| `BIO-I03` Plant, animal, fungal, and microbial physiology | `BIO-B05`, `BIO-I02` | 1. Plant transport/growth; 2. animal organ systems; 3. fungi/decomposition; 4. microbial diversity/metabolism; 5. comparative adaptation | Compare physiological solutions; predict environmental responses; identify symbioses | Microbes are not synonymous with pathogens | ☐ Complete comparative physiology study; ☐ identify unknown strategy from data |
| `BIO-I04` Developmental biology, signaling, regeneration, aging | `BIO-B03`, `BIO-B05` | 1. Cell signaling; 2. patterning/differentiation; 3. morphogenesis; 4. stem cells/regeneration; 5. senescence/aging | Explain development from regulated gene/cell interaction; analyze signaling; compare aging mechanisms | Development is not a preformed miniature simply getting larger | ☐ Interpret perturbation experiment; ☐ map signaling-to-phenotype chain |
| `BIO-I05` Ecology | `BIO-B04`, `BIO-B05`, `STA-B04` | 1. Population growth/life history; 2. competition/predation/mutualism; 3. communities/succession; 4. energy/food webs/cycles; 5. biomes/landscape | Model populations; analyze interactions/food webs; interpret field data | Ecosystems do not have a fixed ideal balance | ☐ Complete field/data study; ☐ predict intervention effects |
| `BIO-A01` Conservation, restoration, global change, human ecology | `BIO-I05`, `ECS-I08` | 1. Extinction risk/genetic diversity; 2. reserve/landscape design; 3. restoration; 4. climate/invasives/harvest; 5. people, justice, governance | Assess threats; design conservation/restoration; evaluate social trade-offs | Excluding people is not the only conservation model | ☐ Produce species/ecosystem plan; ☐ monitor adaptive outcomes |
| `BIO-I06` Immunology, pathogens, host-microbe relations, disease ecology | `BIO-I01`, `BIO-I03` | 1. Barriers/innate immunity; 2. adaptive response/memory; 3. pathogens/virulence; 4. microbiome; 5. transmission/evolution/vaccination | Trace immune responses; explain pathogen evolution; interpret vaccine/infection data | Stronger immune response is not always better; immunopathology can harm | ☐ Analyze immune case; ☐ model transmission/evolution |
| `BIO-I07` Neuroscience | `BIO-I03`, `PHY-B04` | 1. Membrane/electrical signaling; 2. synapses/neurotransmitters; 3. circuits/sensation/action; 4. plasticity/learning; 5. systems/evolution | Explain neural signaling; analyze circuits; connect level of explanation to behavior | A brain region rarely has one isolated psychological function | ☐ Interpret neural experiment; ☐ model simple circuit |
| `BIO-A02` Genomics, epigenetics, systems biology, bioinformatics | `BIO-I01`, `STA-A04`, `CSC-I02` | 1. Sequencing/assembly; 2. annotation/comparative genomics; 3. expression/epigenetics; 4. networks/pathways; 5. pipelines/inference | Analyze omics data; build reproducible pipeline; interpret associations cautiously | Epigenetic does not mean environmentally caused, permanent, or inherited | ☐ Complete public-data analysis; ☐ validate biological claim |
| `BIO-A03` Molecular and cell biology | `BIO-I04`, `CHM-A02` | 1. Genome regulation; 2. protein folding/turnover; 3. trafficking/cytoskeleton; 4. cell cycle/death; 5. quantitative cell methods | Design mechanistic experiments; interpret molecular perturbations; integrate pathways | Molecular diagrams are not static inventories | ☐ Propose/defend mechanism; ☐ analyze multi-assay evidence |
| `BIO-A04` Biotechnology, synthetic biology, gene editing, bioethics | `BIO-A02`, `ENG-I04`, `PHI-I05` | 1. Cloning/expression; 2. CRISPR/editing; 3. circuits/synthetic cells; 4. agriculture/medicine/industry; 5. biosafety, ethics, governance | Design biotech workflow; evaluate off-target/containment; conduct ethical assessment | Ability to edit does not establish desirability or safety | ☐ Build design/risk dossier; ☐ defend governance decision |
| `BIO-A05` Experimental design, model organisms, field methods, inference | `BIO-I05`, `RSH-I04`, `STA-A01` | 1. Biological question/model choice; 2. controls/randomization; 3. lab/field sampling; 4. imaging/molecular/behavioral measures; 5. causal/reproducible inference | Design robust biology studies; choose models; distinguish technical/biological replication | More measurements from one organism are not independent biological replicates | ☐ Preregister experiment; ☐ analyze and reproduce data |
| `BIO-E01` Integrative and frontier biological research | `BIO-A02`, `BIO-A03`, `BIO-A05` | 1. Multi-scale question; 2. literature/model; 3. perturbation/measurement; 4. integrative computation; 5. publication/open artifacts | Conduct original multiscale research; integrate molecular/organism/ecology evidence; defend inference | More omics layers do not automatically yield mechanistic understanding | ☐ Produce original study; ☐ pass replication and oral defense |
