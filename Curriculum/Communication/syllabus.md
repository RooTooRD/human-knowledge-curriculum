# Communication Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `COM-B01` Listening, turn-taking, questions, and repair | `FND-B01`, `FND-I04` | 1. Attending and paraphrasing; 2. Open, closed, probing, and clarifying questions; 3. Turn signals and conversational balance; 4. Mishearing, repair, and confirmation | Summarize another's position before replying; ask diagnostic questions; repair a misunderstanding | Listening is not silent waiting, and paraphrase need not imply agreement | ☐ Reconstruct a five-minute conversation accurately; ☐ Resolve a seeded ambiguity |
| `COM-B02` Audience, intent, context, tone, and nonverbal signals | `COM-B01` | 1. Communicator goals and audience models; 2. Setting, relationship, culture, and stakes; 3. Tone, gesture, posture, gaze, and prosody; 4. Congruence and ambiguity | Adapt message and delivery to context; interpret nonverbal cues probabilistically; separate intent from effect | Body-language cues are not universal mind-reading signals | ☐ Adapt one request to three settings; ☐ Explain two plausible readings of an ambiguous cue |
| `COM-B03` Explanation, demonstration, and storytelling | `COM-B02`, `WRT-B05` | 1. Prior knowledge and explanatory gap; 2. Definition, example, analogy, mechanism, and contrast; 3. Demonstration with checkpoints; 4. Character, stakes, sequence, and takeaway | Explain a process at audience level; demonstrate with verification; use narrative without replacing evidence | A vivid story illustrates but does not establish frequency or cause | ☐ Teach a novice a procedure successfully; ☐ Revise after audience questions |
| `COM-I01` Rhetorical situation, ethos, pathos, logos, and framing | `COM-B02`, `LOG-B02` | 1. Exigence, audience, constraints, and genre; 2. Credibility and identification; 3. Emotion and value; 4. Reasons and evidence; 5. Frames, metaphors, and salience | Analyze persuasive choices; construct an ethical appeal; reframe without falsifying | Rhetoric is not synonymous with deception, and logic alone never removes audience context | ☐ Analyze a speech across all appeals; ☐ Produce two honest frames for one issue |
| `COM-I02` Discussion, disagreement, dialectic, and charity | `COM-B01`, `LOG-B06` | 1. Dialogue goals and norms; 2. Position, interest, evidence, and value; 3. Principle of charity and steelmanning; 4. Burden of proof and productive concession; 5. Impasse and closure | Represent opposing views fairly; locate the exact disagreement; revise beliefs publicly when warranted | Charity does not require accepting weak claims or suppressing moral clarity | ☐ Pass an opponent's summary test; ☐ Conduct a disagreement map |
| `COM-I03` Public speaking, structure, delivery, and anxiety regulation | `COM-B03`, `COM-I01` | 1. Audience outcome and central message; 2. Opening, body, transitions, close; 3. Voice, pace, pause, gesture, and space; 4. Notes, rehearsal, timing, and anxiety; 5. Questions | Deliver a coherent timed speech; use delivery to mark structure; answer questions concisely | Memorizing every word can reduce responsiveness; anxiety need not disappear before effective speaking | ☐ Deliver an evidence-based ten-minute talk; ☐ Improve from recorded feedback |
| `COM-A01` Debate, cross-examination, and impromptu speaking | `COM-I02`, `COM-I03`, `LOG-I05` | 1. Resolution, case, burden, and clash; 2. Evidence comparison and refutation; 3. Cross-examination questions; 4. Flowing and rebuttal; 5. Limited-preparation structure | Build and refute a case; expose assumptions through questions; synthesize a debate by decisive issues | Speed and aggression are not substitutes for warranted clash | ☐ Complete both sides of a formal debate; ☐ Give a structured three-minute impromptu response |
| `COM-I04` Visual communication, charts, slides, and information design | `FND-B09`, `STA-B04`, `WRT-I04` | 1. Visual hierarchy and cognitive load; 2. Chart choice, scales, labels, and uncertainty; 3. Slide-message alignment; 4. Tables, diagrams, annotation, and accessibility; 5. Misleading graphics | Choose representation by question; produce accurate accessible visuals; integrate speech, text, and image | Decoration is not information, and three-dimensional effects often obscure quantities | ☐ Redesign a misleading chart; ☐ Deliver slides usable without reading paragraphs aloud |
| `COM-I05` Media institutions, formats, incentives, and gatekeeping | `FND-I01`, `SOC-B03` | 1. News, entertainment, advertising, public relations, and social media; 2. Ownership, funding, audience, and business models; 3. Selection, editing, ranking, and moderation; 4. Platform algorithms and attention; 5. Public-service and alternative media | Trace how content reaches an audience; compare institutional incentives; distinguish reporting from sponsored persuasion | “The media” is not one coordinated actor, and bias can arise structurally without conspiracy | ☐ Map one story's production chain; ☐ Compare coverage across three media systems |
| `COM-I06` Misinformation, propaganda, persuasion, verification, and platforms | `COM-I01`, `COM-I05`, `LOG-I04` | 1. Error, rumor, disinformation, propaganda, and satire; 2. Emotional and identity mechanisms; 3. Lateral reading, reverse search, provenance, and triangulation; 4. Bots, virality, synthetic media, and recommender systems; 5. Correction | Verify claims and media; identify manipulation techniques; communicate corrections without amplifying harm | Familiarity and repetition are not corroboration; a real image can carry a false caption | ☐ Verify a viral claim with an audit trail; ☐ Design a proportionate correction |
| `COM-A02` Journalism, interviewing, editorial judgment, and media production | `COM-I03`, `COM-I06`, `RSH-I05` | 1. News judgment and public value; 2. Source development and interviewing; 3. Verification, attribution, fairness, and safety; 4. Story forms and multimedia production; 5. Editing, legal risk, corrections, and accountability | Report a sourced story; conduct a responsive interview; make transparent editorial decisions | Quoting two sides is not necessarily fairness when evidence is asymmetric | ☐ Produce a publishable reported story; ☐ Defend source, omission, headline, and correction choices |
| `COM-I07` Negotiation: interests, positions, alternatives, concessions, agreements | `COM-I02`, `PSY-I04` | 1. Preparation, interests, positions, BATNA, and reservation point; 2. Distributive and integrative bargaining; 3. Information exchange, anchoring, and concessions; 4. Objective criteria and package deals; 5. Agreement design and implementation | Prepare and conduct principled negotiation; create mutual gains; assess when to walk away | Compromise is not always value-creating, and cooperation does not mean revealing without strategy | ☐ Complete a scored negotiation simulation; ☐ Write an implementable agreement |
| `COM-A03` Facilitation, mediation, conflict resolution, and difficult conversations | `COM-I07`, `LAW-I07` | 1. Conflict diagnosis and escalation; 2. Process design, ground rules, and participation; 3. Reframing, caucus, and option generation; 4. Identity, emotion, apology, and repair; 5. Settlement, referral, and follow-up | Facilitate balanced deliberation; mediate without deciding merits; conduct a difficult conversation with boundaries | Neutral process does not require neutrality toward abuse or unsafe conduct | ☐ Facilitate a contentious meeting; ☐ Produce a mediation process and safety plan |
| `COM-A04` Cross-cultural, multilingual, organizational, and crisis communication | `COM-I02`, `LIN-I05`, `SOC-I03` | 1. Cultural dimensions and within-group variation; 2. Lingua franca, interpretation, translation, and plain language; 3. Formal/informal organizational channels; 4. Crisis uncertainty, speed, empathy, coordination, and updates; 5. After-action learning | Adapt across cultures without stereotyping; plan multilingual access; communicate uncertainty during crisis | Culture is not destiny, and withholding uncertainty often damages trust more than admitting it | ☐ Create a multilingual crisis brief; ☐ Diagnose a cross-cultural organizational failure |
| `COM-E01` Communication strategy and institutional public reasoning | `COM-A02`, `COM-A03`, `COM-A04` | 1. Stakeholder and information-ecosystem analysis; 2. Objectives, narratives, channels, messengers, and timing; 3. Deliberation, legitimacy, transparency, and trust; 4. Measurement and unintended effects; 5. Institutional learning | Design an ethical communication strategy; align public reasoning with evidence and institutional action; evaluate trust effects | Communication cannot compensate indefinitely for bad policy or inconsistent behavior | ☐ Defend a strategy before adversarial stakeholders; ☐ Evaluate it with behavioral and legitimacy measures |
