# Communication Glossary

These are curriculum-scoped working definitions. They identify how a term is used
in its unit and the operation mastery requires; consult the unit’s advanced
reference for competing, historical, or specialist definitions.

| Unit | Terms |
|---|---|
| `COM-B01` | **Listening:** Within Listening, turn-taking, questions, and conversational repair, the organizing beginner idea that connects attending and paraphrasing with open, closed, probing, and clarifying questions; in this curriculum it supports the ability to summarize another's position before replying.<br>**turn-taking:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through mishearing, repair, and confirmation; mastery includes the ability to ask diagnostic questions. |
| `COM-B02` | **Audience:** Within Audience, intent, context, tone, and nonverbal signals, the organizing beginner idea that connects communicator goals and audience models with setting, relationship, culture, and stakes; in this curriculum it supports the ability to adapt message and delivery to context.<br>**intent:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through congruence and ambiguity; mastery includes the ability to interpret nonverbal cues probabilistically. |
| `COM-B03` | **Explanation:** Within Explanation, demonstration, and storytelling, the organizing beginner idea that connects prior knowledge and explanatory gap with definition, example, analogy, mechanism, and contrast; in this curriculum it supports the ability to explain a process at audience level.<br>**demonstration:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through character, stakes, sequence, and takeaway; mastery includes the ability to demonstrate with verification. |
| `COM-I01` | **Rhetorical situation:** Within Rhetorical situation, ethos, pathos, logos, and framing, the organizing intermediate idea that connects exigence, audience, constraints, and genre with credibility and identification; in this curriculum it supports the ability to analyze persuasive choices.<br>**ethos:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through frames, metaphors, and salience; mastery includes the ability to construct an ethical appeal. |
| `COM-I02` | **Discussion:** Within Discussion, disagreement, dialectic, and intellectual charity, the organizing intermediate idea that connects dialogue goals and norms with position, interest, evidence, and value; in this curriculum it supports the ability to represent opposing views fairly.<br>**disagreement:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through impasse and closure; mastery includes the ability to locate the exact disagreement. |
| `COM-I03` | **Public speaking:** Within Public speaking, speech structure, delivery, and anxiety regulation, the organizing intermediate idea that connects audience outcome and central message with opening, body, transitions, close; in this curriculum it supports the ability to deliver a coherent timed speech.<br>**speech structure:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through questions; mastery includes the ability to use delivery to mark structure. |
| `COM-A01` | **Debate:** Within Debate, cross-examination, and impromptu speaking, the organizing advanced idea that connects resolution, case, burden, and clash with evidence comparison and refutation; in this curriculum it supports the ability to build and refute a case.<br>**cross-examination:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through limited-preparation structure; mastery includes the ability to expose assumptions through questions. |
| `COM-I04` | **Visual communication:** Within Visual communication, charts, slides, and information design, the organizing intermediate idea that connects visual hierarchy and cognitive load with chart choice, scales, labels, and uncertainty; in this curriculum it supports the ability to choose representation by question.<br>**charts:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through misleading graphics; mastery includes the ability to produce accurate accessible visuals. |
