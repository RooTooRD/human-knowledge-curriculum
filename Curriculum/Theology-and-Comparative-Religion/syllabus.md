# Theology and Comparative Religion Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `REL-B01` Sacred, ritual, myth, doctrine, ethics, community, experience | `FND-I03`, `HST-B03` | 1. Defining religion; 2. sacred/myth/symbol; 3. ritual/practice; 4. doctrine/ethics; 5. community/experience | Describe religious phenomena precisely; distinguish dimensions; avoid insider/outsider confusion | Myth in religious studies does not simply mean falsehood | ☐ Analyze a tradition across dimensions; ☐ use neutral and insider vocabulary |
| `REL-B02` Studying religion | `REL-B01`, `RSH-I01` | 1. Theology/religious studies; 2. insider/outsider; 3. history/comparison; 4. social/psychological approaches; 5. reductionism/reflexivity | Match method to question; compare emic/etic accounts; identify reduction | Explaining social function does not exhaust truth or theological meaning | ☐ Analyze one practice by methods; ☐ state method limits |
| `REL-B03` Ancient religions and emergence of major traditions | `REL-B01`, `HST-B05` | 1. Prehistoric/ancient ritual; 2. Mesopotamian/Egyptian/Iranian; 3. South/East Asian formations; 4. Israelite/Jewish and Greco-Roman; 5. late-antique transformations | Place traditions chronologically/geographically; trace continuity/change; interpret sources | “Primitive religion” is a misleading linear-evolution label | ☐ Build comparative timeline; ☐ source-based emergence study |
| `REL-I01` Judaism | `REL-B02`, `REL-B03` | 1. Hebrew Bible/history; 2. covenant/Torah; 3. rabbinic texts/law; 4. philosophy/mysticism/practice; 5. modern movements/diversity | Explain textual/legal development; compare movements; analyze lived practice | Judaism is not only biblical religion or a uniform ethnicity | ☐ Interpret source set; ☐ present historical-practice synthesis |
| `REL-I02` Christianity | `REL-B02`, `REL-B03` | 1. Jesus/New Testament; 2. early church/creeds; 3. sacraments/theology; 4. East/West/reformations; 5. global modern diversity | Trace doctrinal/institutional change; distinguish traditions; contextualize scripture | Christianity is not reducible to one Western denomination | ☐ Compare doctrinal positions; ☐ source-based historical analysis |
| `REL-I03` Hindu traditions | `REL-B02`, `REL-B03` | 1. Vedic traditions; 2. Upanishads/philosophy; 3. epics/Puranas; 4. devotion/ritual/yoga; 5. modern diversity/caste debates | Explain plural texts/practices; compare philosophical schools; avoid homogenization | “Hinduism” names diverse traditions, not one creed/founder | ☐ Comparative text/practice study; ☐ explain historical category |
| `REL-I04` Buddhist traditions | `REL-B02`, `REL-B03` | 1. Buddha/context; 2. teachings/community; 3. Theravada; 4. Mahayana/Vajrayana; 5. practice/global modernity | Explain core teachings and variation; compare schools; analyze meditation ethically | Buddhism is not simply secular mindfulness or uniformly nontheistic philosophy | ☐ Interpret school-specific texts; ☐ compare doctrine/practice |
| `REL-I05` Chinese, Japanese, African, Indigenous, and new traditions | `REL-B02`, `REL-B03` | 1. Confucian/Daoist; 2. Shinto/Japanese syntheses; 3. African traditions; 4. Indigenous traditions; 5. new/syncretic religions | Describe traditions without residual category; analyze syncretism/colonial encounter | Oral or local traditions are not less complex for lacking centralized canon | ☐ Contextual tradition profile; ☐ compare authority/transmission |
| `REL-I06` Theology | `REL-I01`, `REL-I02`, `REL-I03`, `REL-I04` | 1. Revelation/scripture/tradition/reason; 2. ultimate reality/God; 3. creation/humanity; 4. salvation/liberation; 5. eschatology/comparison | Compare theological architectures without flattening; distinguish doctrine from analogy; reason within traditions | Similar vocabulary across religions need not denote identical concepts | ☐ Comparative theology matrix; ☐ defend one internal reasoning chain |
| `REL-I07` Ethics, law, ritual, mysticism, institutions, authority, gender, reform | `REL-I06`, `SOC-B03` | 1. Norm/source; 2. ritual/body; 3. mystical disciplines; 4. office/charisma/community; 5. gender/reform/contest | Analyze authority and change; connect doctrine/practice/institution; represent internal disagreement | Tradition is neither static inheritance nor whatever individuals choose | ☐ Authority/reform case study; ☐ compare lived and official religion |
| `REL-I08` Religion, secularization, nationalism, science, modernity, public life | `REL-I07`, `HST-I05`, `PHI-I03` | 1. Secularization theories; 2. colonialism/modern reform; 3. nation/politics; 4. science/religion; 5. media/market/public sphere | Compare secularisms; analyze public religion; replace simplistic conflict narratives | Modernization has not produced one universal path to religious decline | ☐ Compare two secular orders; ☐ analyze science-religion episode |
| `REL-A01` Comparative theology, philosophy of religion, interreligious reasoning | `REL-I06`, `PHI-I08` | 1. Comparison purpose/ethics; 2. translation/incommensurability; 3. shared questions; 4. learning/critique; 5. dialogue/pluralism | Conduct disciplined comparison; reason across traditions; preserve asymmetry/difference | Dialogue does not require pretending doctrines are identical | ☐ Comparative argument; ☐ moderated interreligious dialogue |
| `REL-A02` Scripture, canon, hermeneutics, criticism, lived interpretation | `REL-I06`, `LIN-I03`, `RSH-I05` | 1. Canon/text history; 2. language/genre; 3. historical criticism; 4. theological hermeneutics; 5. reception/performance | Analyze text historically/literarily/theologically; trace reception; state interpretive premises | Historical context neither exhausts nor automatically invalidates confessional meaning | ☐ Multi-method exegesis; ☐ defend interpretive choices |
| `REL-A03` Religion, conflict, peacebuilding, pluralism, law | `REL-I08`, `POL-I06`, `LAW-A03` | 1. Identity/mobilization; 2. violence/justification; 3. peace traditions/actors; 4. freedom/equality/minority rights; 5. mediation/institutions | Analyze religion in conflict without essentialism; identify peace resources; design pluralist arrangements | Religion is rarely a sole autonomous cause of conflict | ☐ Conflict map; ☐ propose rights-respecting peace design |
| `REL-E01` Advanced theological or religious-studies research | `REL-A01`, `REL-A02`, `RSH-E01` | 1. Tradition/question; 2. languages/sources; 3. theological/historical/comparative method; 4. argument/reflexivity; 5. scholarly/community dissemination | Produce original source-grounded research; distinguish normative/descriptive claims; engage communities ethically | Insider commitment and scholarly rigor are neither identical nor necessarily incompatible | ☐ Complete thesis; ☐ pass academic/community defense |
