# Music Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `MUS-B01` Active listening | `FND-B03`, `PHY-B05` | 1. Pulse/rhythm; 2. pitch/melody; 3. harmony; 4. texture/timbre; 5. form/expression | Identify musical elements by ear; map form; describe without genre prejudice | Listening is an active learned skill, not passive exposure | ☐ Annotated listening exam; ☐ compare performances |
| `MUS-B02` Voice/instrument, posture, practice, ensemble, hearing | `MUS-B01`, `LRN-B06` | 1. Safe technique; 2. tone/intonation; 3. rhythm/coordination; 4. practice/feedback; 5. ensemble/listening/hearing protection | Perform basic repertoire; practice deliberately; coordinate ensemble safely | Repetition without diagnosis can entrench errors | ☐ Perform prepared/unseen piece; ☐ submit practice evidence |
| `MUS-B03` Notation, meter, scales, intervals, chords, keys, ear | `MUS-B01`, `MAT-B03` | 1. Staff/rhythm; 2. intervals/scales; 3. triads/sevenths; 4. key/cadence; 5. sight singing/dictation | Read/write notation; identify intervals/chords; transcribe simple music | Notation is a representation, not music itself | ☐ Theory/aural exam; ☐ notate heard phrase |
| `MUS-I01` Tonal harmony, counterpoint, form, analysis, arrangement, composition | `MUS-B03` | 1. Voice leading; 2. harmonic function/chromaticism; 3. species/free counterpoint; 4. phrase/large form; 5. arrangement/composition | Analyze tonal works; realize harmonies; compose with controlled form | Theory describes practices; it is not universal law of all music | ☐ Harmonic/formal analysis; ☐ compose/arrange work |
| `MUS-I02` Rhythm, tuning, modality, improvisation, orchestration, non-Western systems | `MUS-B02`, `MUS-B03` | 1. Complex rhythm/meter; 2. tuning/temperament; 3. modes/raga/maqam orientation; 4. improvisation; 5. instrumentation/orchestration | Perform/analyze varied systems; improvise within constraints; compare tuning | Western equal temperament is not acoustically or culturally neutral | ☐ Cross-system analysis/performance; ☐ improvised/orchestrated project |
| `MUS-I03` Global music histories | `MUS-B01`, `HST-I11` | 1. Oral/sacred/court; 2. notation/instruments; 3. folk/classical categories; 4. diaspora/exchange; 5. recording/global circulation | Contextualize music socially; trace transmission; analyze categories critically | “Traditional” music changes and innovates | ☐ Contextual listening portfolio; ☐ transmission history |
| `MUS-I04` Western art-music history in global context | `MUS-I01`, `HST-I02` | 1. Medieval/Renaissance; 2. Baroque; 3. Classical/Romantic; 4. modern/contemporary; 5. empire/exchange/institutions | Identify forms/styles; analyze score/context; place canon globally | Canon history is not the whole history of music in Europe | ☐ Score/listening exam; ☐ contextual canon critique |
| `MUS-I05` African, Islamic, South/East/Southeast Asian, Indigenous, Latin traditions | `MUS-I02`, `GEO-I03` | 1. African systems/diaspora; 2. Arab/Turkish/Persian; 3. South Asian; 4. East/Southeast Asian; 5. Indigenous/Latin American | Recognize organizing concepts; compare performance institutions; avoid exoticism | Diverse traditions cannot be compressed into “world music” as one genre | ☐ Tradition-specific studies; ☐ comparative performance analysis |
| `MUS-I06` Jazz, blues, rock, electronic, film, hip-hop, global popular music | `MUS-I03`, `HST-I06` | 1. Blues/jazz; 2. rock/pop; 3. film/media; 4. electronic/dance; 5. hip-hop/global hybrids | Analyze recordings/styles; trace race/class/technology/industry; recognize appropriation/exchange | Popular music is not artistically simple because it is commercial | ☐ Recording analysis; ☐ genre genealogy |
| `MUS-I07` Acoustics, recording, synthesis, production, distribution | `MUS-B03`, `PHY-I03`, `CSC-B04` | 1. Sound/spectrum; 2. microphone/room; 3. digital audio/editing; 4. synthesis/effects/mix; 5. mastering/distribution | Record/mix safely; analyze frequency/time; manage digital workflow | Louder masters are not automatically clearer or better | ☐ Produce recording; ☐ technical listening/measurement test |
| `MUS-I08` Music, ritual, identity, politics, industry, copyright, community | `MUS-I03`, `SOC-B03`, `LAW-I06` | 1. Ritual/community; 2. identity/nation/protest; 3. industry/labor; 4. copyright/ownership; 5. platforms/fandom/public culture | Analyze music as social practice; map rights/revenue; evaluate representation | Musical meaning is not contained solely in sound structure | ☐ Sociomusical case; ☐ rights/industry map |
| `MUS-A01` Performance, improvisation, composition, production, conducting portfolio | `MUS-B02`, `MUS-I01`, `MUS-I07` | 1. Artistic concept; 2. advanced technique; 3. rehearsal/collaboration; 4. production/performance; 5. critique/revision | Deliver coherent artistic portfolio; lead rehearsal; revise from recording/critique | Technical difficulty does not guarantee expressive coherence | ☐ Public portfolio/performance; ☐ process defense |
| `MUS-A02` Musicology, ethnomusicology, analysis, cognition, archives | `MUS-I03`, `RSH-I06`, `COG-I01` | 1. Historical musicology; 2. ethnography; 3. theory/analysis; 4. cognition/perception; 5. archives/editions/ethics | Select methods; analyze sound/text/practice; conduct ethical field/archive work | Musical scores are not complete records of performance/culture | ☐ Research study; ☐ method/source defense |
| `MUS-E01` Original musical or scholarly work | `MUS-A01`, `MUS-A02`, `RSH-E01` | 1. Original question/vision; 2. sustained creation/research; 3. collaboration/evidence; 4. revision/context; 5. performance/publication | Produce original major work; unite craft and inquiry; document/defend contribution | Novel technique alone does not establish artistic or scholarly significance | ☐ Major work/thesis; ☐ expert/public defense |
