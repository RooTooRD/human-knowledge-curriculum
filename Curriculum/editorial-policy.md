# Editorial and Evidence Policy

## Purpose

This policy keeps a very broad curriculum honest about what it knows, what it recommends,
and what still requires evidence. Editorial confidence must follow the warrant of the
claim rather than the prestige of an author, institution, tradition, or technology.

## Claims and warrants

| Claim | Required editorial treatment |
|---|---|
| Repository fact | cite a stable ID or validator result |
| Definition | state scope; distinguish stipulative, technical, historical, and contested use |
| Empirical pattern | identify population, measurement, date, uncertainty, and source |
| Causal explanation | distinguish experiment, quasi-experiment, model, mechanism, and conjecture |
| Historical account | cite primary and secondary evidence; state chronology and historiographical dispute |
| Interpretation | identify text or artifact, context, method, and reasonable rival readings |
| Normative judgment | make premises, affected parties, trade-offs, objections, and conclusion visible |
| Legal rule | state jurisdiction, authority, date, exceptions, and non-advice boundary |
| Medical or safety guidance | use current authoritative guidance and state when professional help is necessary |
| Theological position | identify tradition, school, source hierarchy, method, and internal plurality |
| Future projection | state model, scenario, assumptions, uncertainty, and update trigger |
| Curriculum estimate | state component model and replace it with observed learner evidence when available |

Correlation is not labeled causation. Absence of evidence is not automatically evidence
of absence. A model's precision is not presented as certainty about the world.

## Source selection

Prefer, in order appropriate to the claim:

1. primary texts, data, standards, statutes, cases, official documentation, and original
   research;
2. systematic reviews, scholarly syntheses, critical editions, and authoritative
   reference works;
3. current textbooks and university course materials;
4. high-quality explanatory works whose limitations are explicit.

Prestige is not a substitute for relevance, currency, methodological quality, or
representation of serious disagreement. Resources may be excellent yet inaccessible;
where possible, pair a paid source with a lawful open alternative.

## Citations and quotations

- Link to the most specific authoritative page available.
- Record author or institution, complete title, edition or version, year, and format.
- Give a DOI for research when available and an access or verification date for living
  web resources.
- Cite the source that actually supports the adjacent claim.
- Mark translations and identify the translator or edition.
- Quote only when wording matters; preserve context and observe copyright limits.
- Never invent a page, DOI, quotation, edition, author, or access result.

## Disagreement and plural traditions

Represent a position in terms its informed proponents could recognize. Separate
description from evaluation. In history, philosophy, religion, politics, economics,
law, literature, and the arts, include serious alternatives when the dispute affects
the conclusion. Do not manufacture symmetry when the evidence is highly unequal.

Islamic Studies and comparative religion distinguish confessional claims, academic
historical claims, legal-school positions, theological schools, devotional practice,
and modern political uses. Transliteration and translation choices must remain
consistent within a document and explain consequential variants.

## Accessibility

Source Markdown follows these publication rules:

- one descriptive `#` heading and a logical heading hierarchy;
- descriptive link text rather than repeated “click here” labels;
- simple tables with header rows, used for data rather than page layout;
- prose equivalents immediately before or after diagrams;
- meaningful alternative text for images;
- no required distinction made only through color, position, sound, or typography;
- expanded abbreviations on first use and nearby glossary access;
- captions and transcripts for project-created audiovisual instruction;
- equations accompanied by a verbal account where the reasoning is not recoverable
  from notation alone.

These rules apply the relevant principles of
[WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), the W3C
[page-structure guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/page-structure/), and
[tables tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/tables/). A Markdown source audit
does not establish conformance of every renderer; the deployed publication needs
keyboard, reflow, contrast, zoom, screen-reader, and mobile testing.

## Safety, law, medicine, and professional boundaries

Learning materials may explain dangerous systems without authorizing unsupervised
practice. Projects must prefer simulations, inert materials, sandboxes, public data,
and supervised facilities. Stop conditions and escalation routes belong in the task,
not in a footnote after it.

Medical, mental-health, financial, and legal sections are educational. They must not
diagnose an individual, create a professional relationship, or obscure local law and
qualified judgment.

## AI-assisted production

An AI-assisted contribution records the system, date, task, files affected, and human
verification. The contributor remains responsible for every source, calculation, unit
ID, interpretation, and safety implication. AI output is not a source. Sensitive data
must not be disclosed to an unapproved service.

## Maintenance

Review living links and edition-sensitive resources annually. Prioritize fast-changing
law, policy, health, AI, security, climate, finance, standards, and software. Record
substantive changes in the plan and validation report, rerun every validator, and retain
stable IDs or publish a migration note.
