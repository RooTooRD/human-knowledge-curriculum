# Standards and Source Frameworks

Phase 1 uses professional frameworks as coverage checks, not as authorities that define
the whole curriculum. Most are degree-oriented; this project extracts their durable
knowledge structure while adding civic, practical, historical, religious, and
interdisciplinary knowledge.

## Source policy for later phases

- Prefer primary professional standards and official university materials.
- Record edition and publication year for every book.
- Date-check law, policy, software, AI, medicine, climate, and finance resources.
- Distinguish a primary source, textbook, lecture course, exercise bank, reference work,
  and encyclopedia; no single resource should be mislabeled as all six.
- Preserve scholarly disagreement instead of declaring a contested school canonical.

## Phase 3 resource provenance

Phase 3 applies the following source hierarchy:

1. official publisher or author pages for edition-sensitive books;
2. official university course pages and open-course collections;
3. official intergovernmental and professional references for health, climate,
   security, law, economics, and education;
4. established, editorially controlled open textbooks and encyclopedias;
5. creator-owned educational sites for selected visual or video explanations.

The [shared resource catalog](resource-catalog.md) records cross-disciplinary living
resources once. Discipline catalogs own book metadata and field-specific selections.
Every recommendation is a best-fit editorial judgment, not a universal ranking.

The principal open-course and reference systems checked for this release were MIT
OpenCourseWare, Open Yale Courses, NPTEL, OpenStax, the Stanford Encyclopedia of
Philosophy, NCBI Bookshelf, WHO, IPCC, NASA Science, NIST CSRC, Smarthistory, CORE
Econ, and the Cornell Legal Information Institute. Their living pages are dated in the
catalog and must be rechecked annually.
