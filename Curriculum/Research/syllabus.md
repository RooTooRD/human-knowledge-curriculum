# Research Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `RSH-B01` Inquiry, answerable questions, constructs, and scope | `FND-B10`, `LOG-B01` | 1. Curiosity to problem; 2. Descriptive, causal, interpretive, evaluative, design questions; 3. Constructs and units; 4. Scope, feasibility, stakes | Formulate bounded answerable questions; identify constructs and unit of analysis; justify significance | A broad topic is not a research question | ☐ Turn five topics into viable questions; ☐ defend scope and answer type |
| `RSH-B02` Search, discovery, libraries, databases, references, and citation trails | `FND-I01`, `WRT-B04` | 1. Search concepts and synonyms; 2. catalogs, scholarly databases, repositories; 3. Boolean, field, cited/citing search; 4. source capture and deduplication; 5. stopping rules | Execute reproducible searches; follow citation networks; maintain a source library | One search engine does not cover the literature, and first-page results are not a sample | ☐ Deliver a search log and bibliography; ☐ reproduce another's search |
| `RSH-B03` Observation, hypothesis, model, prediction, test, and explanation | `RSH-B01`, `LOG-B05` | 1. Observation and anomaly; 2. hypothesis and alternative; 3. models and assumptions; 4. risky predictions and tests; 5. explanation and revision | Build a testable model; derive discriminating predictions; revise from evidence | Confirmation of a prediction does not uniquely prove a hypothesis | ☐ Create a hypothesis-model-prediction chain; ☐ design a rival-sensitive test |
| `RSH-B04` Research ethics, consent, integrity, authorship, and conflicts | `RSH-B01`, `PHI-B04` | 1. Harm, benefit, justice, respect; 2. informed consent and vulnerable participants; 3. fabrication, falsification, plagiarism; 4. authorship, credit, conflicts; 5. review and reporting | Identify ethical issues; construct consent and data protections; allocate credit transparently | Ethics approval does not eliminate the researcher's ongoing responsibility | ☐ Analyze ethics cases; ☐ write a proportionate ethics plan |
| `RSH-I01` Source types, authority, and provenance | `RSH-B02`, `FND-I03` | 1. Primary/secondary/tertiary by question; 2. genre, venue, peer review; 3. author expertise and incentives; 4. provenance, version, retraction; 5. triangulation | Select sources by evidential role; verify provenance; weigh authority without appeal-to-authority error | “Primary” does not mean unbiased or superior for every question | ☐ Evaluate a mixed source packet; ☐ trace one claim to origin |
| `RSH-I02` Literature review, synthesis, disagreement, and gaps | `RSH-I01`, `WRT-I01` | 1. Review question and boundaries; 2. coding concepts/methods/results; 3. synthesis by theme and claim; 4. disagreement and heterogeneity; 5. genuine gaps and contribution | Build a synthesis matrix; explain consensus/dispute; distinguish missing study from important gap | Listing sources sequentially is not synthesis | ☐ Write a claim-centered review; ☐ justify a tractable contribution |
| `RSH-I03` Operationalization, validity, reliability, sampling, and measurement | `RSH-B03`, `STA-B02` | 1. Construct to indicator; 2. content/criterion/construct validity; 3. reliability and error; 4. sampling and coverage; 5. measurement invariance and reactivity | Design and critique measures; estimate reliability; align sample to inference | Reliability does not guarantee validity | ☐ Operationalize one construct three ways; ☐ validate and critique a measure |
| `RSH-I04` Experimental and quasi-experimental design | `RSH-I03`, `STA-I03` | 1. Treatment, control, assignment; 2. blinding, placebo, compliance; 3. factorial/repeated/field designs; 4. quasi-experimental contrasts; 5. validity, power, and analysis plan | Design controlled studies; identify validity threats; preregister analysis | Random assignment balances in expectation, not perfectly in every sample | ☐ Produce a powered design; ☐ critique a quasi-experiment |
| `RSH-I05` Historical, archival, textual, and comparative methods | `RSH-I01`, `HST-B02` | 1. Archive construction and source survival; 2. internal/external criticism; 3. close/contextual reading; 4. case comparison and process tracing; 5. narrative and uncertainty | Build source-based interpretations; corroborate and contextualize; reason from absences cautiously | Archives are curated remnants, not transparent records of the past | ☐ Complete a source dossier; ☐ defend a comparative historical claim |
| `RSH-I06` Interviews, observation, ethnography, case study, and qualitative analysis | `RSH-I03`, `SOC-B02` | 1. Sampling and access; 2. interview guides and probing; 3. observation/fieldnotes and reflexivity; 4. cases and within-case evidence; 5. coding, themes, negative cases, saturation | Collect ethical qualitative evidence; code transparently; support interpretations with cases | Small samples are not automatically invalid when inference is analytic rather than prevalence-based | ☐ Conduct/code pilot interviews; ☐ show theme and disconfirming case |
| `RSH-A01` Mixed methods, triangulation, case selection, and integration | `RSH-I04`, `RSH-I05`, `RSH-I06` | 1. Complementarity and design rationale; 2. sequential/convergent/embedded designs; 3. case selection; 4. triangulation and divergence; 5. integrated inference | Select methods by question; integrate rather than append findings; explain divergence | Multiple methods do not guarantee truth when they share bias | ☐ Design a mixed-method study; ☐ produce an integrated joint display |
| `RSH-A02` Reproducibility, replication, open science, data and code stewardship | `RSH-I04`, `STA-I06`, `CSC-I06` | 1. Computational and empirical reproducibility; 2. preregistration and registered reports; 3. versioned data/code/environment; 4. replication types; 5. FAIR access, privacy, and preservation | Package reproducible work; plan meaningful replication; balance openness with rights | Reproduction of output does not validate the study's assumptions | ☐ Independent rerun succeeds; ☐ create a stewardship/replication plan |
| `RSH-A03` Systematic review, meta-analysis, evidence grading, and publication bias | `RSH-I02`, `STA-I03` | 1. Protocol and eligibility; 2. comprehensive search/screening; 3. extraction and bias assessment; 4. effect synthesis/heterogeneity; 5. reporting bias and certainty | Conduct protocol-driven review; synthesize appropriately; grade certainty | Meta-analysis cannot rescue incomparable or systematically biased studies | ☐ Complete a mini systematic review; ☐ justify synthesis and certainty |
| `RSH-A04` Philosophy of method, underdetermination, paradigms, and programs | `RSH-B03`, `PHI-I03`, `HST-I07` | 1. Induction, falsification, confirmation; 2. theory-ladenness and underdetermination; 3. paradigms and revolutions; 4. research programs and pluralism; 5. values and objectivity | Compare philosophies against practice; identify hidden methodological commitments; defend critical pluralism | Paradigm dependence does not entail that evidence is powerless | ☐ Analyze one scientific controversy; ☐ defend a methodology with limitations |
| `RSH-A05` Peer review, grants, publication, and scholarly communities | `RSH-I02`, `WRT-A01`, `RSH-B04` | 1. Venue and audience; 2. constructive review; 3. response and revision; 4. grant aims, significance, method, budget; 5. incentives, credit, and community norms | Review rigorously; answer reviews; produce a fundable proposal; navigate incentives ethically | Peer review is quality control, not certification of truth | ☐ Complete double-role mock review; ☐ revise proposal from panel feedback |
| `RSH-E01` Independent original research and dissemination | `RSH-A01`, `RSH-A02`, `RSH-A04`, `RSH-A05` | 1. Original question and contribution; 2. protocol, ethics, feasibility; 3. evidence production and analysis; 4. robustness and limitation; 5. article, data/code, presentation, public translation | Execute defensible original inquiry; make calibrated claims; disseminate reproducibly to expert/public audiences | Novelty does not excuse weak method or exaggerated significance | ☐ Pass oral defense and peer review; ☐ release an auditable research package |
