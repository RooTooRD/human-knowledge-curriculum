# Learning Syllabus

## `LRN-B01` — Goals, motivation, identity, and study environment

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B04`, `FND-B05`  
**Lessons:** 1. Values and learning goals; 2. Intrinsic/extrinsic motivation, expectancy, and value; 3. Identity, self-efficacy, and attribution; 4. Environmental cues, friction, materials, and accountability.  
**Learning outcomes:** Formulate a meaningful, measurable learning goal; redesign a study environment around predictable behavior; distinguish a strategy failure from a fixed-ability judgment.  
**Common misconceptions:** Motivation need not precede action, and describing oneself as “not a math/language person” is not an explanation of current performance.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can produce a four-week goal with process metrics. - [ ] Can remove three sources of friction and measure the result.

## `LRN-B02` — Attention, distraction, rest, and cognitive load

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-B01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Selective and sustained attention; 2. Task switching, interruption, devices, and mind wandering; 3. Working-memory limits and intrinsic/extraneous load; 4. Breaks, sleep, exercise, stress, and study blocks.  
**Learning outcomes:** Diagnose attentional failure; segment a complex task to fit working memory; choose rest and environment interventions appropriate to the cause.  
**Common misconceptions:** Multitasking usually means costly switching, and longer uninterrupted study is not automatically more productive.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can run and interpret a one-week distraction log. - [ ] Can redesign one overloaded lesson into manageable steps.

## `LRN-B03` — Encoding, working memory, and long-term memory

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B05`, `LRN-B02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Sensory, working, and long-term memory; 2. Attention, elaboration, organization, and dual coding; 3. schemas, chunks, and prior knowledge; 4. Forgetting, interference, reconstruction, and cues.  
**Learning outcomes:** Explain how information becomes retrievable knowledge; choose encoding strategies for verbal, spatial, and procedural content; predict likely interference.  
**Common misconceptions:** Memory is reconstructive rather than a recording, and repeated viewing creates familiarity more readily than durable recall.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can encode and recall an unfamiliar concept using elaboration and organization. - [ ] Can explain a memory error using a specific mechanism.

## `LRN-B04` — Note-taking, annotation, and knowledge organization

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B05`, `LRN-B02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Capture versus thinking; 2. Annotation for questions, structure, claims, and evidence; 3. Outline, matrix, mapping, and worked-example notes; 4. Source metadata, review cues, and note maintenance.  
**Learning outcomes:** Select a note form for the material and task; produce notes that preserve source and argument structure; convert notes into retrieval prompts.  
**Common misconceptions:** More transcription is not necessarily better, and visually elaborate notes may conceal weak organization or recall.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can create a one-page synthesis from a lecture and reading. - [ ] Can answer self-generated questions without reopening the source.

## `LRN-B05` — Retrieval practice, spacing, and interleaving

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-B03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Recall, recognition, and testing effect; 2. Expanding and fixed spacing; 3. Interleaving versus blocked practice; 4. Flashcards, free recall, practice tests, and scheduling.  
**Learning outcomes:** Design retrieval prompts that require production; schedule review before and after likely forgetting; interleave problem types without destroying initial comprehension.  
**Common misconceptions:** Difficult retrieval can strengthen memory, and rereading's fluency is not a reliable measure of learning.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can build and follow a spaced review set for one unit. - [ ] Can demonstrate delayed recall under closed-book conditions.

## `LRN-B06` — Practice, feedback, error correction, and desirable difficulty

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-B03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Knowledge, skill, and performance decomposition; 2. Worked examples, guided practice, and fading; 3. Immediate/delayed, outcome/process feedback; 4. Error classification, correction, and reattempt.  
**Learning outcomes:** Decompose a skill into trainable components; request or generate actionable feedback; maintain an error log that changes subsequent practice.  
**Common misconceptions:** Practice does not make perfect when it stabilizes errors, and struggle is useful only when paired with attainable success and feedback.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can correct and re-solve a previously missed problem without cues. - [ ] Can show a declining frequency for one error class.

## `LRN-I01` — Concept maps, atomic notes, synthesis notes, and external memory

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-B04`, `LRN-B05`  
**Lessons:** 1. Concepts, propositions, hierarchy, and cross-links; 2. Atomic claims and permanent notes; 3. Synthesis across sources; 4. Indexes, tags, links, versioning, and retrieval.  
**Learning outcomes:** Build a concept map that exposes dependency and misconception; write source-grounded atomic and synthesis notes; retrieve related ideas without over-tagging.  
**Common misconceptions:** A graph of links is not knowledge unless links express meaningful relations, and atomizing notes can destroy context.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can reconstruct a domain outline from the note system. - [ ] Can trace every synthesis claim to its sources.

## `LRN-I02` — Metacognition, calibration, and illusions of competence

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-B05`, `LRN-B06`  
**Lessons:** 1. Monitoring and control; 2. predictions, confidence, and calibration; 3. Fluency, familiarity, hindsight, and answer-copying illusions; 4. Closed-book checks and strategy revision.  
**Learning outcomes:** Compare predicted and observed performance; identify a misleading subjective cue; allocate study based on evidence rather than comfort.  
**Common misconceptions:** Low confidence can accompany accurate performance and high confidence can accompany shallow familiarity.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can produce a calibration chart from repeated predictions. - [ ] Can replace one ineffective strategy using performance data.

## `LRN-I03` — Planning, deliberate practice, and progress measurement

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-B01`, `LRN-I02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Backward planning from performance; 2. Baseline, target, leading and lagging indicators; 3. Deliberate practice at the edge of competence; 4. Weekly review, bottlenecks, plateaus, and replanning.  
**Learning outcomes:** Convert an outcome into sequenced practice; choose measures resistant to vanity; revise a plan after diagnosing the limiting factor.  
**Common misconceptions:** Hours alone do not measure progress, and deliberate practice is focused correction rather than any difficult repetition.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can produce and execute a measured six-week plan. - [ ] Can justify one plan revision from observed data.

## `LRN-I04` — Transfer, analogy, abstraction, and varied practice

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-B06`, `LRN-I02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Near and far transfer; 2. Surface and structural similarity; 3. Analogy mapping and limits; 4. Contrasting cases, varied contexts, abstraction, and self-explanation.  
**Learning outcomes:** Identify the deep structure shared by two problems; generate an analogy and state where it fails; vary practice to improve recognition of applicability.  
**Common misconceptions:** Transfer is not automatic, and one vivid analogy can mislead when its relational structure differs.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can solve a novel-context problem and name the transferred principle. - [ ] Can distinguish two surface-similar problems requiring different methods.

## `LRN-I05` — Collaborative learning, explanation, teaching, and peer review

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-B06`, `FND-I04`  
**Lessons:** 1. Joint goals, roles, norms, and psychological safety; 2. Elaboration through explanation and questioning; 3. Peer instruction and reciprocal teaching; 4. Criteria-based review, disagreement, revision, and accountability.  
**Learning outcomes:** Explain a concept responsively to another learner; structure collaboration so every member thinks; give specific, usable, respectful feedback.  
**Common misconceptions:** Group work is not collaborative learning when one person performs the cognition, and teaching an error confidently reinforces it.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can lead a peer explanation with diagnostic questions. - [ ] Can show a revision directly attributable to peer feedback.

## `LRN-A01` — Expertise, mental models, tacit knowledge, and plateaus

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-I03`, `LRN-I04`  
**Lessons:** 1. Novice/expert representation and chunking; 2. Mental models, conditional knowledge, and pattern recognition; 3. Tacit skill, apprenticeship, and deliberate articulation; 4. Plateaus, automaticity, adaptive expertise, and overlearning.  
**Learning outcomes:** Compare novice and expert representations of a task; externalize part of a tacit process; diagnose whether a plateau needs volume, variation, feedback, or model change.  
**Common misconceptions:** Expertise is domain-specific and can produce blind spots; automatic speed alone is not adaptive expertise.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can elicit and model an expert workflow. - [ ] Can break a documented plateau using a targeted intervention.

## `LRN-A02` — Independent curriculum design and resource evaluation

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-I01`, `LRN-A01`, `RSH-I02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Scope, terminal performances, and dependency mapping; 2. Coverage versus depth and core versus extension; 3. Resource authority, pedagogy, exercises, accessibility, and fit; 4. Sequencing, assessment gates, review, and revision.  
**Learning outcomes:** Build an acyclic curriculum for a bounded goal; evaluate resources against explicit criteria; identify and repair hidden prerequisite gaps.  
**Common misconceptions:** A bibliography is not a curriculum, and following one expert's course order does not guarantee fit for a different learner or goal.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can publish a dependency-driven six-month curriculum. - [ ] Can defend every included and excluded unit.

## `LRN-A03` — Long-term retention, review systems, and knowledge maintenance

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-I01`, `LRN-I03`, `LRN-A01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Retention horizon and acceptable forgetting; 2. Review queues, cumulative tests, projects, and teaching; 3. Stable versus perishable knowledge; 4. Archiving, updating, pruning, and reactivation after lapse.  
**Learning outcomes:** Design different maintenance policies for facts, skills, models, and changing information; audit a review burden; reactivate dormant knowledge efficiently.  
**Common misconceptions:** Maintaining everything at equal recall is impossible and undesirable, and a large flashcard queue can crowd out synthesis and use.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can operate a sustainable annual review system. - [ ] Can document one successful reactivation and one justified retirement.

## `LRN-E01` — Science of learning research and instructional experimentation

**Prerequisites:** `LRN-A01`, `PSY-A03`, `RSH-A03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Major learning theories and mechanisms; 2. Laboratory, classroom, and ecological evidence; 3. Measurement, boundary conditions, moderators, and publication bias; 4. Single-case and group instructional experiments; 5. Translation from evidence to practice.  
**Learning outcomes:** Critically synthesize a learning-science literature; design an ethical instructional experiment; distinguish established effect, plausible mechanism, and context-sensitive recommendation.  
**Common misconceptions:** Average effects do not prescribe identical practice for every learner, and a neuroscience label does not strengthen weak educational evidence.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can produce a systematic evidence brief with uncertainty. - [ ] Can preregister and analyze a small instructional experiment.
