# Learning Glossary

These are curriculum-scoped working definitions. They identify how a term is used
in its unit and the operation mastery requires; consult the unit’s advanced
reference for competing, historical, or specialist definitions.

| Unit | Terms |
|---|---|
| `LRN-B01` | **Goals:** Within Goals, motivation, identity, and study environment, the organizing beginner idea that connects values and learning goals with intrinsic/extrinsic motivation, expectancy, and value; in this curriculum it supports the ability to formulate a meaningful, measurable learning goal.<br>**motivation:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through environmental cues, friction, materials, and accountability; mastery includes the ability to redesign a study environment around predictable behavior. |
| `LRN-B02` | **Attention:** Within Attention, distraction, rest, and cognitive load, the organizing beginner idea that connects selective and sustained attention with task switching, interruption, devices, and mind wandering; in this curriculum it supports the ability to diagnose attentional failure.<br>**distraction:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through breaks, sleep, exercise, stress, and study blocks; mastery includes the ability to segment a complex task to fit working memory. |
| `LRN-B03` | **Encoding:** Within Encoding, working memory, and long-term memory, the organizing beginner idea that connects sensory, working, and long-term memory with attention, elaboration, organization, and dual coding; in this curriculum it supports the ability to explain how information becomes retrievable knowledge.<br>**working memory:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through forgetting, interference, reconstruction, and cues; mastery includes the ability to choose encoding strategies for verbal, spatial, and procedural content. |
| `LRN-B05` | **Retrieval practice:** Within Retrieval practice, spacing, and interleaving, the organizing beginner idea that connects recall, recognition, and testing effect with expanding and fixed spacing; in this curriculum it supports the ability to design retrieval prompts that require production.<br>**spacing:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through flashcards, free recall, practice tests, and scheduling; mastery includes the ability to schedule review before and after likely forgetting. |
| `LRN-B06` | **Practice:** Within Practice, feedback, error correction, and desirable difficulty, the organizing beginner idea that connects knowledge, skill, and performance decomposition with worked examples, guided practice, and fading; in this curriculum it supports the ability to decompose a skill into trainable components.<br>**feedback:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through error classification, correction, and reattempt; mastery includes the ability to request or generate actionable feedback. |
| `LRN-B04` | **Note-taking:** Within Note-taking, annotation, and knowledge organization, the organizing beginner idea that connects capture versus thinking with annotation for questions, structure, claims, and evidence; in this curriculum it supports the ability to select a note form for the material and task.<br>**annotation:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through source metadata, review cues, and note maintenance; mastery includes the ability to produce notes that preserve source and argument structure. |
| `LRN-I01` | **Concept maps:** Within Concept maps, atomic notes, synthesis notes, and external memory, the organizing intermediate idea that connects concepts, propositions, hierarchy, and cross-links with atomic claims and permanent notes; in this curriculum it supports the ability to build a concept map that exposes dependency and misconception.<br>**atomic notes:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through indexes, tags, links, versioning, and retrieval; mastery includes the ability to write source-grounded atomic and synthesis notes. |
| `LRN-I02` | **Metacognition:** Within Metacognition, calibration, and illusions of competence, the organizing intermediate idea that connects monitoring and control with predictions, confidence, and calibration; in this curriculum it supports the ability to compare predicted and observed performance.<br>**calibration:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through closed-book checks and strategy revision; mastery includes the ability to identify a misleading subjective cue. |
| `LRN-I03` | **Planning:** Within Planning, deliberate practice, and progress measurement, the organizing intermediate idea that connects backward planning from performance with baseline, target, leading and lagging indicators; in this curriculum it supports the ability to convert an outcome into sequenced practice.<br>**deliberate practice:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through weekly review, bottlenecks, plateaus, and replanning; mastery includes the ability to choose measures resistant to vanity. |
| `LRN-I04` | **Transfer:** Within Transfer, analogy, abstraction, and varied practice, the organizing intermediate idea that connects near and far transfer with surface and structural similarity; in this curriculum it supports the ability to identify the deep structure shared by two problems.<br>**analogy:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through contrasting cases, varied contexts, abstraction, and self-explanation; mastery includes the ability to generate an analogy and state where it fails. |
| `LRN-I05` | **Collaborative learning:** Within Collaborative learning, explanation, teaching, and peer review, the organizing intermediate idea that connects joint goals, roles, norms, and psychological safety with elaboration through explanation and questioning; in this curriculum it supports the ability to explain a concept responsively to another learner.<br>**explanation:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through criteria-based review, disagreement, revision, and accountability; mastery includes the ability to structure collaboration so every member thinks. |
| `LRN-A01` | **Expertise:** Within Expertise, mental models, tacit knowledge, and plateaus, the organizing advanced idea that connects novice/expert representation and chunking with mental models, conditional knowledge, and pattern recognition; in this curriculum it supports the ability to compare novice and expert representations of a task.<br>**mental models:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through plateaus, automaticity, adaptive expertise, and overlearning; mastery includes the ability to externalize part of a tacit process. |
| `LRN-A02` | **Independent curriculum design evaluation:** Within Independent curriculum design and resource evaluation, the organizing advanced idea that connects scope, terminal performances, and dependency mapping with coverage versus depth and core versus extension; in this curriculum it supports the ability to build an acyclic curriculum for a bounded goal.<br>**resource evaluation:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through sequencing, assessment gates, review, and revision; mastery includes the ability to evaluate resources against explicit criteria. |
| `LRN-A03` | **Long-term retention:** Within Long-term retention, review systems, and knowledge maintenance, the organizing advanced idea that connects retention horizon and acceptable forgetting with review queues, cumulative tests, projects, and teaching; in this curriculum it supports the ability to design different maintenance policies for facts, skills, models, and changing information.<br>**review systems:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through archiving, updating, pruning, and reactivation after lapse; mastery includes the ability to audit a review burden. |
| `LRN-E01` | **Science of learning research experimentation:** Within Science of learning research and instructional experimentation, the organizing expert idea that connects major learning theories and mechanisms with laboratory, classroom, and ecological evidence; in this curriculum it supports the ability to critically synthesize a learning-science literature.<br>**instructional experimentation:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through translation from evidence to practice; mastery includes the ability to design an ethical instructional experiment. |
