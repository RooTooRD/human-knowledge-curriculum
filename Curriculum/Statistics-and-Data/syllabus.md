# Statistics and Data Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `STA-B01` Data, variables, cases, measurement, and generation | `FND-I02`, `FND-I03` | 1. Questions, cases, variables, values; 2. Categorical/quantitative and discrete/continuous data; 3. Units, coding, metadata, and provenance; 4. Population, process, and data-generating mechanism | Define an analysis-ready data structure; classify variables; trace observations to generation | Data are produced, not simply “found,” and numbers can be category labels | ☐ Create a data dictionary; ☐ Explain the generating process for a dataset |
| `STA-B02` Sampling, bias, missingness, observational studies, and experiments | `STA-B01`, `RSH-B01` | 1. Target population and frame; 2. Random, stratified, cluster, convenience samples; 3. Nonresponse and missingness; 4. Observational/experimental designs; 5. Bias and generalization | Evaluate sample/design fit; identify selection and missing-data threats; delimit generalization | Large biased samples remain biased, and random sampling differs from random assignment | ☐ Critique three study samples; ☐ design a defensible sampling plan |
| `STA-B04` Distributions, summaries, visualization, and robust description | `STA-B01`, `MAT-B03` | 1. Frequency and distribution shape; 2. Center, spread, quantiles; 3. Histograms, boxplots, scatterplots, and tables; 4. Outliers and robust summaries; 5. Multivariate comparison | Select/compute summaries and plots; describe shape and association without causal language | Mean is not always typical, and axis choices can distort apparent change | ☐ Produce an honest exploratory report; ☐ explain why chosen summaries fit |
| `STA-B03` Chance, conditional probability, independence, and Bayes | `MAT-B03`, `MAT-B08` | 1. Sample spaces and events; 2. Addition/multiplication rules; 3. Conditional probability and independence; 4. Total probability and Bayes; 5. Trees, tables, and simulation | Compute probabilities; translate among representations; update beliefs from base rates and evidence | Disjoint events are not independent, and inverse conditional probabilities differ | ☐ Solve compound/Bayesian problems; ☐ validate by simulation |
| `STA-I01` Random variables and distributions | `STA-B03`, `MAT-I04` | 1. Discrete/continuous random variables; 2. CDF, density, mass; 3. Expectation, variance, covariance; 4. Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson, normal, exponential; 5. Transformations and joint distributions | Derive moments; choose distributions from assumptions; calculate joint/conditional quantities | Density is not probability at a point, and zero covariance need not mean independence | ☐ Derive and compute distribution properties; ☐ justify a model choice |
| `STA-I02` Laws of large numbers, central limit theorem, and simulation | `STA-I01`, `CSC-B03` | 1. Sampling distributions; 2. LLN and stabilization; 3. CLT and standard error; 4. Monte Carlo simulation; 5. Approximation conditions and convergence diagnostics | Explain repeated-sampling behavior; use CLT appropriately; implement and validate simulation | CLT does not make raw data normal, and many simulations do not fix a wrong model | ☐ Demonstrate LLN/CLT computationally; ☐ quantify simulation error |
| `STA-I03` Estimation, intervals, testing, effect size, and power | `STA-B02`, `STA-I02` | 1. Estimands and estimators; 2. Bias, variance, consistency; 3. Confidence intervals; 4. Hypotheses, p-values, errors; 5. Effect size, multiplicity, power, and sample size | Conduct and interpret inference; separate practical/statistical significance; plan power | A p-value is not the probability the null is true, and 95% confidence is procedural | ☐ Analyze a study with interval/effect/power; ☐ correct common interpretations |
| `STA-I04` Linear, generalized linear, and multilevel regression | `STA-I03`, `MAT-I06` | 1. Linear model and least squares; 2. Diagnostics, transformations, interactions; 3. Logistic/count models; 4. Hierarchical data and partial pooling; 5. Prediction versus explanation | Fit, diagnose, and interpret regression; encode interactions; respect model scope | A coefficient is not automatically causal, and high fit need not mean valid prediction | ☐ Complete a regression analysis with diagnostics; ☐ explain each coefficient correctly |
| `STA-I05` Bayesian modeling | `STA-I01`, `STA-I03` | 1. Prior, likelihood, posterior; 2. Conjugacy and computation; 3. Credible intervals and posterior prediction; 4. Hierarchical models; 5. Model checking, sensitivity, and decision | Build/update Bayesian models; interpret posterior quantities; check prior/model sensitivity | Priors need not be arbitrary subjectivity, and credible/confidence intervals differ | ☐ Derive a simple posterior; ☐ fit and critique a Bayesian model |
| `STA-I06` Data cleaning, provenance, formats, querying, and reproducibility | `STA-B04`, `CSC-B04` | 1. Tidy/relational structures and schemas; 2. Parsing, types, joins, reshape; 3. Missing, duplicate, inconsistent, anomalous values; 4. SQL/query workflows; 5. scripts, environments, lineage, and reports | Build an auditable cleaning pipeline; query/join safely; reproduce results from raw data | Cleaning is not neutral deletion, and spreadsheets are not reliable provenance systems | ☐ Transform messy raw data reproducibly; ☐ pass independent rerun and audit |
| `STA-A01` Causal inference | `STA-I04`, `LOG-I05`, `RSH-I03` | 1. Counterfactuals and estimands; 2. DAGs, backdoors, colliders; 3. Randomization and natural experiments; 4. Matching, weighting, IV, discontinuity, difference-in-differences; 5. sensitivity and transport | State causal assumptions; identify effects; choose/design estimators; perform sensitivity analysis | Controlling every variable can induce bias, and causal diagrams do not create identification | ☐ Identify and estimate a causal effect; ☐ defend assumptions and sensitivity |
| `STA-A02` Time series, spatial, survival, and longitudinal data | `STA-I04`, `STA-I05` | 1. Dependence and repeated measures; 2. Trend, seasonality, ARIMA/state space; 3. Spatial autocorrelation and geostatistics; 4. censoring, hazards, survival; 5. longitudinal mixed models | Model dependence correctly; forecast with uncertainty; interpret hazards and spatial effects | Independent-data methods understate uncertainty, and hazard is not probability | ☐ Analyze one dependent-data type; ☐ validate forecast/model assumptions |
| `STA-A03` Nonparametric, resampling, robust, and high-dimensional methods | `STA-I03`, `MAT-A01` | 1. Rank and distribution-free methods; 2. bootstrap and permutation; 3. robust estimators; 4. smoothing and density; 5. multiple testing and high dimension | Select assumption-light methods; construct resampling inference; assess robustness | “Nonparametric” does not mean assumption-free, and bootstrap cannot repair unrepresentative data | ☐ Implement bootstrap/permutation analysis; ☐ compare classical and robust results |
| `STA-A04` Statistical learning, regularization, validation, and decisions | `STA-I04`, `MAT-A05`, `CSC-I03` | 1. Prediction loss and generalization; 2. train/validation/test and cross-validation; 3. ridge, lasso, elastic net; 4. trees, ensembles, kernels; 5. calibration, thresholds, decision costs | Build leakage-free predictive workflows; tune/compare models; choose decisions from costs | Test data used repeatedly cease to be a test, and accuracy can hide class imbalance | ☐ Produce a reproducible benchmark; ☐ justify metric, split, calibration, threshold |
| `STA-A05` Complex data types | `STA-I04`, `STA-I06` | 1. Survey weights and complex samples; 2. administrative linkage; 3. sensor/stream data; 4. text/image features; 5. network observations and dependence | Adapt data models to collection structure; document linkage/measurement error; avoid naïve independence | Convenient digital traces need not represent people or constructs | ☐ Analyze one complex dataset; ☐ produce a provenance and bias audit |
| `STA-A06` Privacy, fairness, uncertainty communication, and ethics | `STA-A01`, `PHI-I05`, `SEC-I02` | 1. Confidentiality, reidentification, minimization; 2. disclosure control and differential privacy; 3. fairness definitions and trade-offs; 4. uncertainty/risk communication; 5. governance, consent, and harm | Conduct ethical data-risk review; calculate/interpret privacy trade-offs; compare fairness criteria | Deidentification is not anonymity, and fairness metrics cannot resolve normative choices alone | ☐ Audit a data product; ☐ communicate limitations to affected stakeholders |
| `STA-E01` Mathematical statistics and asymptotic theory | `STA-A03`, `MAT-A09` | 1. Sufficiency, completeness, exponential families; 2. decision theory and optimality; 3. likelihood and estimating equations; 4. asymptotic convergence; 5. empirical processes orientation | Prove estimator/test properties; derive asymptotic distributions; compare optimal procedures | Asymptotic validity need not imply good finite-sample behavior | ☐ Complete advanced derivations; ☐ verify theory with simulation |
| `STA-E02` Original statistical methodology and collaboration | `STA-A01`, `STA-A04`, `STA-E01` | 1. Domain problem and estimand; 2. literature and methodological gap; 3. new model/estimator; 4. theory, simulation, and software; 5. application, communication, and reproducibility | Co-develop a defensible method; establish properties; implement and communicate it | Mathematical novelty without a real inferential problem is not sufficient method research | ☐ Produce reproducible original work; ☐ pass domain and statistical peer review |
