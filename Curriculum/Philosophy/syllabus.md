# Philosophy Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PHI-B01` Questions, conceptual analysis, dialogue, ways of life | `LOG-B01`, `HST-B03` | 1. Wonder/aporia; 2. concept/counterexample; 3. argument/dialogue; 4. thought experiment; 5. philosophy as practice across traditions | Formulate philosophical problems; analyze concepts; conduct charitable dialogue | Philosophy is not merely personal opinion or quotation of famous thinkers | ☐ Write conceptual analysis; ☐ lead Socratic dialogue |
| `PHI-B02` Knowledge | `PHI-B01`, `LOG-B03` | 1. Belief/truth/justification; 2. Gettier and analysis; 3. skepticism; 4. perception/memory/testimony; 5. disagreement/virtue | Evaluate knowledge claims; compare sources; respond to skepticism | Certainty is not required for all knowledge | ☐ Analyze epistemic case; ☐ defend calibrated belief |
| `PHI-B03` Reality | `PHI-B01`, `LOG-B05` | 1. Object/property; 2. identity/change; 3. time/space; 4. causation/law; 5. freedom/possibility | Compare metaphysical positions; test with cases; expose modal assumptions | Abstract metaphysical claims still require arguments and explanatory costs | ☐ Defend view against counterexample; ☐ map dependence relations |
| `PHI-B04` Value, action, virtue, duty, consequence, care, pluralism | `PHI-B01`, `FND-B04` | 1. Good/right/virtue; 2. consequentialism; 3. deontology; 4. virtue/care; 5. pluralism/dilemma | Analyze actions across theories; distinguish agent/act/outcome; make justified judgment | Moral disagreement does not make all judgments equally warranted | ☐ Resolve case across frameworks; ☐ defend and qualify conclusion |
| `PHI-I01` Rational choice, action, responsibility, practical reason | `PHI-B04`, `STA-B03` | 1. Desire/reason/action; 2. intention/weakness; 3. utility/risk; 4. freedom/responsibility; 5. collective action | Analyze reasons and agency; compare choice models; assess responsibility under constraint | Explaining behavior causally does not automatically excuse it | ☐ Analyze responsibility case; ☐ critique rational-choice model |
| `PHI-I02` Mind | `PHI-B03`, `PSY-B02` | 1. Dualism/physicalism; 2. behavior/function; 3. consciousness/qualia; 4. self/embodiment; 5. other minds | Compare mind theories; evaluate thought experiments/evidence; avoid category errors | Correlation between brain and mind does not by itself settle ontology | ☐ Debate mind case; ☐ connect empirical and conceptual evidence |
| `PHI-I03` Philosophy of science | `PHI-B02`, `RSH-B03`, `HST-I07` | 1. Induction/falsification; 2. explanation/law; 3. model/idealization; 4. realism/underdetermination; 5. values/demarcation | Analyze scientific inference; evaluate models; distinguish methodological questions | No single simple “scientific method” covers every science | ☐ Analyze research episode; ☐ defend realism/anti-realism position |
| `PHI-A01` Philosophy of mathematics, logic, probability, computation | `PHI-I03`, `LOG-A02`, `MAT-A08` | 1. Platonism/formalism/structuralism; 2. logical consequence; 3. probability interpretations; 4. computation/information; 5. limits/objectivity | Compare foundations; connect formal results to claims; avoid overreading limits | Gödel results do not prove machines lack intelligence or truth is relative | ☐ Advanced position paper; ☐ defend interpretation of formal theorem |
| `PHI-I04` Philosophy of language | `PHI-B02`, `LIN-I03` | 1. Sense/reference; 2. descriptions/names; 3. truth/meaning; 4. use/speech acts; 5. interpretation/metaphor | Analyze meaning theories; distinguish sentence/speaker meaning; evaluate interpretation | Meaning is neither only dictionary definition nor private intention | ☐ Analyze contested utterance; ☐ compare two semantic theories |
| `PHI-I05` Applied ethics | `PHI-B04`, `SYS-I05` | 1. Stakeholders/harms; 2. technology/media; 3. environment; 4. research/business; 5. war/profession | Conduct structured ethical assessment; compare duties/outcomes/justice; propose safeguards | Ethics is not a final compliance checkbox | ☐ Produce ethics case report; ☐ defend mitigation/accountability |
| `PHI-A02` Bioethics and philosophy of medicine | `PHI-I05`, `HLT-I02` | 1. Health/disease/disability; 2. consent/capacity; 3. reproduction/genetics; 4. allocation/public health; 5. death/care | Analyze clinical/policy dilemmas; question concepts of normality; balance autonomy/justice | Disability is not reducible to biological defect | ☐ Ethics consultation; ☐ defend public-health trade-off |
| `PHI-A03` AI, information, and digital ethics | `PHI-I05`, `AIX-B01`, `SEC-I02` | 1. Agency/responsibility; 2. privacy/surveillance; 3. algorithmic justice; 4. autonomy/manipulation; 5. governance/future risk | Assign responsibility in systems; evaluate algorithmic power; design governance | Calling a system autonomous does not erase human/institutional accountability | ☐ Audit AI case; ☐ propose defensible governance |
| `PHI-I06` Social and political philosophy | `PHI-B04`, `HST-I03` | 1. Authority/consent; 2. liberty/rights; 3. equality/justice; 4. property/markets; 5. power/oppression/democracy | Compare political principles; apply to institutions; expose ideal/nonideal assumptions | Equal treatment can reproduce inequality when circumstances differ | ☐ Design/critique institution; ☐ defend justice theory |
| `PHI-I07` Aesthetics | `PHI-B01`, `ART-B01` | 1. Art definition; 2. beauty/taste; 3. representation/expression; 4. interpretation/intention; 5. value/canon/institution | Argue aesthetic judgments; compare interpretation theories; analyze art institutions | Subjective experience does not make criticism reasonless | ☐ Write close philosophical critique; ☐ defend interpretation |
| `PHI-I08` Philosophy of religion | `PHI-B02`, `REL-B02` | 1. Divine concepts; 2. cosmological/design/ontological arguments; 3. evil/hiddenness; 4. faith/reason/experience; 5. pluralism/religious language | Reconstruct arguments fairly; distinguish logical/evidential problems; compare epistemologies | Philosophy of religion is neither preaching nor automatic debunking | ☐ Analyze major argument; ☐ conduct cross-position oral exam |
| `PHI-A04` Ancient and global classical traditions | `PHI-B01`, `HST-I01` | 1. Greek/Roman; 2. Indian; 3. Chinese; 4. Islamic/African; 5. medieval exchange | Read traditions contextually/comparatively; trace concepts; resist false equivalence | Global traditions are not footnotes to a single European sequence | ☐ Comparative primary-text essay; ☐ defend translation/context |
| `PHI-A05` Early modern to nineteenth century | `PHI-A04`, `HST-I03` | 1. Rationalism/empiricism; 2. social contract; 3. Kant; 4. idealism/Marx; 5. utilitarianism/Nietzsche and modernity | Reconstruct systems; connect historical problems; compare responses | Canonical labels can hide disagreement within each thinker | ☐ Primary-text examination; ☐ trace one problem across period |
| `PHI-A06` Analytic, phenomenological, existential, pragmatist, critical, feminist, postcolonial | `PHI-A05`, `HST-I05` | 1. Analytic turns; 2. phenomenology/existentialism; 3. pragmatism; 4. critical/feminist; 5. postcolonial/global debates | Compare methods; apply contemporary theories; identify shared problems | Analytic/continental is an imperfect institutional divide, not two coherent doctrines | ☐ Cross-tradition debate; ☐ advanced interpretive essay |
| `PHI-A07` Metaethics, normative theory, moral psychology | `PHI-B04`, `PHI-I01`, `PSY-I04` | 1. Realism/expressivism; 2. reasons/motivation; 3. advanced consequence/duty/virtue; 4. intuition/emotion; 5. disagreement/progress | Distinguish meta/normative/descriptive claims; build moral theory; use psychology without reduction | Describing moral cognition does not settle what is right | ☐ Construct theory/case set; ☐ answer debunking challenge |
| `PHI-E01` Independent philosophical research and synthesis | `PHI-A01`, `PHI-A06`, `RSH-E01` | 1. Original problem; 2. literature/argument map; 3. thesis/objections; 4. interdisciplinary evidence; 5. article/defense | Produce original rigorous argument; anticipate strongest objections; integrate evidence appropriately | Novel terminology is not a contribution without clarified problem and argument | ☐ Publishable paper; ☐ adversarial oral defense |
