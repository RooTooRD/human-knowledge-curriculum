# History Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `HST-B01` Time, chronology, periodization, change, continuity, cause, contingency | `FND-B07`, `LOG-B05` | 1. Chronology/timeline; 2. period labels; 3. change/continuity; 4. multi-causation; 5. contingency/counterfactual restraint | Build chronology; justify periodization; weigh causes without inevitability | Later outcomes were not inevitable to historical actors | ☐ Construct analytical timeline; ☐ defend causal hierarchy |
| `HST-B02` Primary sources, context, provenance, corroboration, silence, anachronism | `HST-B01`, `RSH-I01` | 1. Source/event distinction; 2. creator/audience/purpose; 3. context/language; 4. corroboration/conflict; 5. survival/silence | Critique sources; corroborate; infer cautiously from absence | Primary sources are not direct unbiased windows | ☐ Complete source dossier; ☐ state warranted and unwarranted inferences |
| `HST-B03` Narrative, comparison, interpretation, evidence argument | `HST-B02`, `WRT-I01` | 1. Event/selection; 2. narrative sequence; 3. comparison; 4. thesis/evidence; 5. rival interpretation | Write evidence-grounded narrative/argument; compare fairly; revise from counterevidence | History is neither mere facts nor unconstrained storytelling | ☐ Write comparative essay; ☐ defend against rival account |
| `HST-B04` Origins, migration, foraging, agriculture, cities, writing, early states | `HST-B01`, `BIO-B04`, `GEO-B01` | 1. Human evolution/migration; 2. foraging societies; 3. domestication; 4. settlement/city; 5. states/writing/inequality | Explain transitions with regional variation; use archaeology/genetics; reject progress inevitability | Agriculture did not instantly or universally improve health/freedom | ☐ Compare two transitions; ☐ analyze evidence limits |
| `HST-B05` Ancient Afro-Eurasian and American civilizations to c. 500 CE | `HST-B04`, `GEO-B04` | 1. Mesopotamia/Egypt/Africa; 2. South/East Asia; 3. Mediterranean/Persia; 4. Americas; 5. empires, trade, thought, religion | Compare states/societies; trace connections; locate developments geographically | “Ancient civilization” was not confined to Greece and Rome | ☐ Comparative civilization atlas; ☐ source-based thematic essay |
| `HST-I01` Post-classical worlds, c. 500–1500 | `HST-B05`, `REL-B03` | 1. Byzantium/Europe; 2. Islamic worlds; 3. African states/networks; 4. South/East/Central Asia; 5. Americas/oceanic exchange | Explain regional systems and connections; compare institutions/religions; challenge “dark age” frame | 500–1500 was not globally stagnant or isolated | ☐ Build connected-world map; ☐ compare three regions |
| `HST-I02` Connected early modern world, c. 1450–1750 | `HST-I01`, `GEO-I01` | 1. Gunpowder/land empires; 2. maritime expansion; 3. Columbian exchange; 4. slavery/commerce; 5. reform/science/state formation | Trace global exchanges and coercion; compare empires; explain uneven change | European expansion was neither immediate total dominance nor peaceful exchange | ☐ Analyze commodity/empire chain; ☐ write connected history |
| `HST-I03` Revolutions and industrial transformations, c. 1750–1914 | `HST-I02`, `ECO-B02` | 1. Enlightenment/revolutions; 2. industrialization; 3. capitalism/labor; 4. nationalism/state; 5. abolition/reform/resistance | Compare revolutions; explain industrial divergence; connect ideas/material change | Industrialization was not one event or uniformly beneficial | ☐ Comparative revolution essay; ☐ analyze industrial evidence |
| `HST-I04` Imperialism, nationalism, world wars, genocide, mass society, c. 1870–1945 | `HST-I03`, `POL-B02` | 1. New imperialism; 2. mass politics/nationalism; 3. World War I; 4. interwar ideologies/crisis; 5. World War II/Holocaust/genocides | Explain war/empire with multiple scales; analyze perpetration/resistance; use victim-centered sources | War did not arise from one cause, and genocide was not spontaneous chaos | ☐ Causal war analysis; ☐ ethically sourced genocide study |
| `HST-I05` Cold War, decolonization, development, rights, globalization, 1945–1991 | `HST-I04`, `POL-I06` | 1. Bipolar order/nuclear age; 2. Asian/African/Middle Eastern decolonization; 3. development/nonalignment; 4. rights/social movements; 5. globalization/Soviet collapse | Analyze local agency within Cold War; compare decolonization; trace global institutions | Postcolonial history was not merely a superpower proxy story | ☐ Compare decolonization paths; ☐ analyze Cold War local conflict |
| `HST-I06` Contemporary world since 1991 | `HST-I05`, `COM-I05` | 1. Post-Cold-War orders; 2. wars/terror/security; 3. digital/global economy; 4. migration/populism/power shifts; 5. climate/pandemic/current-history limits | Build evidence-based recent chronology; distinguish history from journalism; analyze competing periodizations | Recency and abundant data do not eliminate archival gaps or hindsight | ☐ Produce sourced contemporary dossier; ☐ defend provisional interpretation |
| `HST-I07` History of knowledge, science, medicine, technology, education | `HST-I02`, `RSH-B03` | 1. Knowledge institutions/traditions; 2. Islamic/Asian/European exchanges; 3. scientific revolution debate; 4. industrial/modern science; 5. medicine/technology/education/publics | Trace knowledge across cultures/institutions; contextualize discovery; connect tools and power | Science was not created by one civilization or lone-genius sequence | ☐ Trace one concept globally; ☐ analyze institution/instrument |
| `HST-I08` Economic, labor, business, material history | `HST-I03`, `ECO-B01` | 1. Household/subsistence; 2. trade/money/finance; 3. slavery/serfdom/wage labor; 4. firms/industry/consumption; 5. inequality/material life | Use quantitative/material sources; compare labor regimes; connect macro and household | Markets and capitalism are not timeless identical systems | ☐ Analyze prices/wages/artifacts; ☐ write labor-regime comparison |
| `HST-I09` Political, legal, military, diplomatic, institutional history | `HST-I01`, `POL-B01` | 1. State/administration; 2. law/court; 3. armies/war; 4. diplomacy/order; 5. citizenship/resistance | Reconstruct institutions; distinguish formal rule/practice; analyze war and diplomacy | Political history is not only rulers and battles | ☐ Institutional case study; ☐ connect policy to lived effects |
| `HST-I10` Social, family, gender, demographic, environmental, everyday history | `HST-I01`, `SOC-B01` | 1. Family/kinship; 2. gender/sexuality; 3. class/race/status; 4. demography/disease; 5. environment/daily life | Recover nonelite experience; use demographic/material evidence; analyze categories historically | Modern social categories cannot be projected unchanged backward | ☐ Microhistory from sparse sources; ☐ contextualize category |
| `HST-I11` Intellectual, religious, literary, artistic, cultural history | `HST-I01`, `PHI-B01`, `REL-B02` | 1. Concepts/context; 2. texts/reception; 3. religious change; 4. arts/literature; 5. print/media/public culture | Contextualize ideas without reducing them; trace reception; integrate form/institution | Context influences meaning but does not make argument content irrelevant | ☐ Contextual reading; ☐ reception-history project |
| `HST-A01` Historiography | `HST-B03`, `RSH-I05` | 1. Narrative/positivism; 2. social/Annales/Marxist; 3. cultural/gender/postcolonial; 4. micro/global/connected; 5. digital/quantitative debates | Compare schools; locate intervention; choose method by question | New historiography supplements and revises rather than simply replacing all prior work | ☐ Historiographic essay; ☐ position a research project |
| `HST-A02` Public history, heritage, museums, monuments, memory | `HST-A01`, `COM-I06`, `PHI-I05` | 1. Collective memory/history; 2. museum/exhibition; 3. monument/commemoration; 4. heritage/provenance/restitution; 5. digital/public participation | Curate evidence responsibly; analyze memory conflict; design inclusive interpretation | Removing, retaining, or contextualizing monuments are distinct policy choices | ☐ Build public-history prototype; ☐ conduct stakeholder/ethics review |
| `HST-A03` Comparative civilization, periodization critique, world synthesis | `HST-I06`, `HST-I08`, `HST-I11` | 1. Civilization concept/critique; 2. comparison/connection; 3. empire/religion/economy; 4. divergence/convergence; 5. global periodization/synthesis | Synthesize without teleology; compare commensurably; center interaction and internal diversity | Civilizations are not sealed, homogeneous actors | ☐ Write global synthesis; ☐ defend units/periods |
| `HST-E01` Original historical research | `HST-A01`, `HST-A02`, `RSH-E01` | 1. Question/archive; 2. source corpus/criticism; 3. historiographic contribution; 4. narrative/argument; 5. public/scholarly dissemination | Produce original historical knowledge; handle silences/conflict; write ethical argument | Archival novelty alone is not significance | ☐ Complete thesis/source apparatus; ☐ pass scholarly/public defense |
