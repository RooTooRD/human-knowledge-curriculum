# Sociology and Anthropology Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SOC-B01` Society, culture, norms, roles, status, identity, socialization | `FND-I03`, `HST-B03` | 1. Social relation/structure; 2. culture/meaning; 3. norms/sanctions; 4. role/status/identity; 5. socialization/agency | Analyze behavior socially; distinguish norm/value/role; explain identity formation | Social construction does not mean imaginary or freely chosen | ☐ Analyze everyday institution; ☐ compare two cultural contexts |
| `SOC-B02` Questions, comparison, fieldwork, reflexivity | `SOC-B01`, `RSH-B01` | 1. Sociological imagination; 2. emic/etic; 3. comparison; 4. fieldwork/positionality; 5. ethics/reflexivity | Formulate social questions; compare without ethnocentrism; state researcher position | Cultural relativism for understanding does not prohibit ethical evaluation | ☐ Write reflexive field memo; ☐ design comparative question |
| `SOC-B03` Institutions | `SOC-B01`, `FND-I05` | 1. Family/kinship; 2. religion/education; 3. economy/work; 4. polity/law/media; 5. medicine/institutional interaction | Map institutional rules/roles; analyze coordination/conflict; trace institutional change | Institutions are more than buildings or formal organizations | ☐ Map life event across institutions; ☐ identify reinforcing rules |
| `SOC-I01` Social theory | `SOC-B01`, `HST-I03` | 1. Marx/Durkheim/Weber; 2. interaction/action; 3. functional/conflict; 4. structure/agency; 5. power/field/network | Reconstruct theories; apply comparatively; derive testable/interpretive implications | Theory is not merely opinion or jargon | ☐ Analyze one case with three theories; ☐ defend best explanatory fit |
| `SOC-I02` Biological/cultural anthropology, archaeology, language, variation | `SOC-B02`, `BIO-B04`, `LIN-B01` | 1. Primate/human evolution; 2. culture/subsistence/kinship; 3. archaeology/material inference; 4. language/culture; 5. race and human variation | Integrate biological/cultural evidence; interpret artifacts; reject racial essentialism | Biological variation does not divide humanity into discrete natural races | ☐ Analyze archaeological/variation evidence; ☐ explain biocultural process |
| `SOC-I03` Groups, organizations, networks, interaction, deviance, collective behavior | `SOC-B03`, `PSY-I04` | 1. Interaction/self; 2. groups/norms; 3. organizations/bureaucracy; 4. networks/diffusion; 5. deviance/crowds | Analyze micro-macro links; map network/organization; explain labeling/control | Deviance is defined partly by social response, not act alone | ☐ Conduct interaction/organization study; ☐ analyze network diffusion |
| `SOC-I04` Stratification and intersectionality | `SOC-B01`, `HST-I10` | 1. Class/status/mobility; 2. race/ethnicity/caste; 3. gender/sexuality; 4. disability; 5. intersection/power | Measure inequality; distinguish categories/mechanisms; analyze intersecting structures | Group disparities neither prove individual deficiency nor one single cause | ☐ Interpret mobility/disparity data; ☐ produce mechanism-rich analysis |
| `SOC-I05` Population, fertility, mortality, migration, urbanization, transition | `SOC-B03`, `STA-B04` | 1. Demographic measures; 2. fertility/mortality; 3. transition/age structure; 4. migration; 5. urbanization/projection | Calculate demographic rates; read pyramids; build cohort/projection explanations | Population momentum can continue after fertility falls | ☐ Analyze census data; ☐ produce bounded projection |
| `SOC-I06` Work, technology, consumption, media, globalization, change | `SOC-B03`, `HST-I06`, `CSC-A06` | 1. Labor/process/profession; 2. automation/platforms; 3. consumption/status; 4. media/network society; 5. globalization/resistance | Analyze sociotechnical change; trace labor/value; evaluate platform effects | Technology does not determine social outcomes independently of institutions | ☐ Study one technology transition; ☐ map winners/losers/power |
| `SOC-I07` Ethnographic, interview, survey, comparative, historical, network methods | `SOC-B02`, `RSH-I06`, `STA-B02` | 1. Ethnography; 2. interview/focus group; 3. survey; 4. comparative/historical; 5. network/mixed methods | Select method by inference; collect/analyze ethically; triangulate | Qualitative and quantitative methods answer different but overlapping questions | ☐ Complete mixed pilot; ☐ defend inference limits |
| `SOC-A01` Political sociology | `SOC-I01`, `POL-I03` | 1. State/class; 2. nationalism/citizenship; 3. movements; 4. revolution/contention; 5. civil society/public sphere | Explain political mobilization; compare state-society relations; analyze movement outcomes | Grievance alone does not explain mobilization | ☐ Comparative movement study; ☐ model political opportunity/network |
| `SOC-A02` Economic sociology | `SOC-I01`, `ECO-I03` | 1. Embedded markets; 2. networks/trust; 3. firms/professions; 4. valuation/finance; 5. institutions/performativity | Analyze markets as social institutions; explain valuation; compare with economic models | Prices emerge within legal/cultural/network structures, not social vacuum | ☐ Study a market institution; ☐ integrate economic/social explanation |
| `SOC-A03` Science, knowledge, health, education, environment, digital society | `SOC-I06`, `RSH-A04` | 1. Knowledge/expertise; 2. science/technology; 3. medicine/health; 4. education; 5. environment/digital systems | Analyze expertise/institution; trace category/standard effects; evaluate inequality | Studying social production of knowledge does not show factual claims are arbitrary | ☐ Institutional knowledge case; ☐ analyze classification consequences |
| `SOC-A04` Decolonial, feminist, interpretive, critical, global theory | `SOC-I01`, `HST-I05` | 1. Coloniality/decolonization; 2. feminist standpoint/intersection; 3. interpretation/meaning; 4. critical theory/hegemony; 5. global South/connected theory | Compare critiques; locate standpoint/power; apply without reducing evidence to identity | Standpoint can reveal blind spots without making every claim equally valid | ☐ Compare theoretical texts; ☐ conduct reflexive application |
| `SOC-E01` Original social research and theory building | `SOC-I07`, `SOC-A04`, `RSH-E01` | 1. Social puzzle; 2. theory/concepts; 3. comparative/mixed evidence; 4. negative cases/reflexivity; 5. public/scholarly return | Produce original social explanation; revise theory from evidence; return findings ethically | Rich description alone is not theory, while abstraction without cases is empty | ☐ Complete thesis/artifacts; ☐ pass participant/peer defense |
