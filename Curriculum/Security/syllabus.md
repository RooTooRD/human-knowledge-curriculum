# Security Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SEC-B01` Assets, threats, vulnerabilities, controls, risk, and adversaries | `FND-I04`, `STA-B03` | 1. Asset and mission; 2. threat/hazard/adversary; 3. vulnerability/exposure; 4. likelihood, impact, uncertainty; 5. prevent, detect, respond, recover controls | Build a basic risk model; prioritize controls; state residual risk | Risk is not eliminated, and possibility alone does not determine priority | ☐ Complete household/digital risk register; ☐ justify top mitigations |
| `SEC-B02` Personal digital hygiene | `SEC-B01`, `FND-B08` | 1. Password managers and MFA; 2. updates and least privilege; 3. backup/recovery; 4. phishing and malicious files; 5. privacy settings and account recovery | Secure accounts/devices; recognize social attacks; test recovery | Complex reused passwords are weaker than unique managed ones | ☐ Harden an account/device checklist; ☐ restore from tested backup |
| `SEC-B03` Physical, household, travel, and emergency security | `SEC-B01`, `LIF-B04` | 1. Situational awareness without hypervigilance; 2. access, lighting, fire, valuables; 3. travel documents/comms; 4. evacuation/shelter; 5. proportional response | Assess physical risks; make layered plans; act safely during emergencies | Security theater and constant fear do not equal effective risk reduction | ☐ Conduct site/travel assessment; ☐ complete scenario drill |
| `SEC-I01` Confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity, accountability | `SEC-B01`, `CSC-B01` | 1. Security properties; 2. identity/authentication/authorization; 3. trust boundaries; 4. logging/nonrepudiation; 5. trade-offs and assurance | Translate needs into properties; identify violated property; design layered control | Encryption alone does not provide integrity, availability, or correct authorization | ☐ Analyze breaches by property; ☐ write security requirements |
| `SEC-I02` Privacy, identity, surveillance, consent, and protection | `SEC-I01`, `LAW-B03` | 1. Personal data and identity; 2. collection, inference, linkage; 3. surveillance actors/effects; 4. consent, minimization, purpose limitation; 5. rights and privacy engineering | Map data flows; assess privacy harm; propose minimization/governance | “Nothing to hide” ignores power, context, inference, and collective effects | ☐ Complete privacy impact assessment; ☐ redesign a data flow |
| `SEC-I03` Cryptographic concepts | `SEC-I01`, `MAT-B02` | 1. Threat models and Kerckhoffs principle; 2. symmetric/asymmetric encryption; 3. hashes/MACs/signatures; 4. key exchange, certificates, PKI; 5. randomness, storage, protocol failure | Choose primitives by goal; explain key lifecycle; avoid common misuse | Encoding and hashing are not encryption, and strong primitives can form weak protocols | ☐ Analyze a protocol transcript; ☐ implement/use a vetted library correctly |
| `SEC-I04` System, application, network, cloud, and supply-chain security | `SEC-I01`, `CSC-I06`, `CSC-I07` | 1. Hardening/isolation; 2. input, auth, session, web flaws; 3. segmentation/filtering; 4. cloud identity/configuration; 5. dependency/build/update trust | Find attack surfaces; apply secure configuration; trace supply-chain risk | A firewall does not secure vulnerable applications or trusted dependencies | ☐ Harden/test a lab system; ☐ produce attack-surface inventory |
| `SEC-A01` Threat modeling, secure design, testing, and vulnerability management | `SEC-I04`, `CSC-I10` | 1. Assets/actors/abuse cases; 2. trust/data-flow diagrams; 3. secure patterns; 4. review, SAST/DAST/fuzz/pentest; 5. triage, remediation, disclosure | Produce actionable threat model; validate controls; prioritize vulnerabilities by exploit path | Severity scores alone do not determine contextual risk | ☐ Threat-model and test an application; ☐ verify remediation |
| `SEC-A02` Incident response, forensics, continuity, recovery, and crisis exercises | `SEC-I04`, `COM-A04` | 1. Preparation/roles; 2. detection/triage/containment; 3. evidence and forensics; 4. eradication/recovery/continuity; 5. communication and lessons | Lead structured response; preserve evidence; recover and learn | Rapidly wiping systems can destroy evidence and impede eradication | ☐ Run tabletop/live exercise; ☐ produce timeline and corrective plan |
| `SEC-I05` Human factors, social engineering, insider risk, and culture | `SEC-B02`, `PSY-I04` | 1. Attention, trust, urgency, authority; 2. phishing/pretext/physical tactics; 3. usable security; 4. insider pathways and safeguards; 5. reporting culture and training | Design human-centered controls; conduct ethical simulations; distinguish error, negligence, and malice | Users are not simply the “weakest link”; system design shapes behavior | ☐ Redesign a risky workflow; ☐ evaluate training behaviorally |
| `SEC-A03` Governance, assurance, standards, audit, and economics | `SEC-A01`, `LAW-I06`, `ECO-I03` | 1. Policy, ownership, risk appetite; 2. control frameworks/standards; 3. evidence, audit, certification; 4. third-party risk; 5. incentives, externalities, investment | Build governance/control program; evaluate assurance evidence; allocate security resources | Compliance is not security, and certification is time-bounded evidence | ☐ Map risks to tested controls; ☐ defend investment portfolio |
| `SEC-A04` Intelligence, military strategy, deterrence, terrorism, and hybrid conflict | `POL-I06`, `HST-I06`, `LAW-A03` | 1. Intelligence cycle and uncertainty; 2. force, strategy, operations; 3. deterrence/coercion; 4. terrorism/insurgency; 5. cyber, information, proxy, gray-zone conflict | Evaluate intelligence; connect means to political ends; compare deterrence conditions | Tactical success does not guarantee strategic success | ☐ Write a sourced strategic assessment; ☐ red-team escalation paths |
| `SEC-A05` Safety engineering, catastrophic risk, and critical infrastructure | `SEC-A02`, `SYS-I05`, `ENG-I02` | 1. Hazard analysis and defense-in-depth; 2. human/technical/common-cause failure; 3. infrastructure interdependence; 4. catastrophic and tail risk; 5. resilience, drills, governance | Perform hazard analysis; model cascading failure; design resilient controls | Rare events with high consequence cannot be dismissed by frequency alone | ☐ Complete bow-tie/FMEA scenario; ☐ test a continuity architecture |
| `SEC-E01` Security research, red teaming, and strategic foresight | `SEC-A01`, `SEC-A03`, `SEC-A04` | 1. Research ethics/scope; 2. adversarial hypothesis and attack path; 3. controlled exploitation; 4. systemic/strategic scenarios; 5. disclosure, mitigation, validation | Discover and validate novel weakness; emulate adversaries safely; communicate calibrated risk | Red teaming without rules, evidence, and remediation becomes uncontrolled harm | ☐ Deliver reproducible finding/exercise; ☐ pass remediation readback |
