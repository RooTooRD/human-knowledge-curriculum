# Logic Syllabus

## `LOG-B01` — Claims, questions, definitions, and arguments

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B05`, `FND-I03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Declarative, interrogative, imperative, and expressive utterances; 2. Claims and truth conditions; 3. Lexical, stipulative, precising, and theoretical definitions; 4. Argument versus explanation, report, illustration, and persuasion.  
**Learning outcomes:** Classify utterances by logical role; test definitions for scope and circularity; distinguish an argument from neighboring forms of discourse.  
**Common misconceptions:** A heated disagreement need not contain an argument, and a dictionary definition does not settle every conceptual dispute.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can annotate claims, questions, definitions, and arguments in unfamiliar prose. - [ ] Can repair an overbroad, narrow, or circular definition.

## `LOG-B02` — Premises, conclusions, inference indicators, and argument maps

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-B01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Explicit and implicit premises; 2. Conclusion and premise indicators; 3. Serial, convergent, linked, and divergent support; 4. Standard form and argument mapping.  
**Learning outcomes:** Reconstruct an argument charitably in standard form; supply a necessary implicit premise; draw a map preserving support relations.  
**Common misconceptions:** Sentence order need not match inferential order, and two premises listed together need not work jointly.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can standardize and map a complex editorial argument. - [ ] Can defend each supplied implicit premise as necessary and plausible.

## `LOG-B03` — Validity, soundness, strength, cogency, and counterexample

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-B02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Necessary versus probable support; 2. Validity and invalidating interpretations; 3. Soundness and premise truth; 4. Inductive strength and cogency; 5. Counterexample construction.  
**Learning outcomes:** Evaluate deductive and inductive arguments using the correct standards; construct a counterexample to an invalid form; separate premise criticism from inference criticism.  
**Common misconceptions:** A valid argument may have false premises and conclusion, while a true conclusion does not rescue bad reasoning.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can classify twenty arguments with written justification. - [ ] Can construct counterexamples rather than merely assert invalidity.

## `LOG-B04` — Ambiguity, vagueness, equivocation, and operational definition

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-B01`, `FND-B06`  
**Lessons:** 1. Lexical and syntactic ambiguity; 2. Borderline cases and sorites structure; 3. Equivocation across an inference; 4. Precising and operational definitions; 5. Costs of measurement proxies.  
**Learning outcomes:** Generate and disambiguate multiple readings; locate a meaning shift that invalidates an argument; operationalize a construct while stating what the operation misses.  
**Common misconceptions:** Vagueness is not the same as ambiguity, and operationalization does not prove that a measure exhausts the concept.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can expose and repair an equivocal argument. - [ ] Can propose two operational definitions and compare their validity.

## `LOG-B05` — Deduction, induction, abduction, and analogy

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-B02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Deductive entailment; 2. Enumerative and statistical induction; 3. Inference to the best explanation; 4. Source/target mapping in analogy; 5. Choosing an inference type.  
**Learning outcomes:** Identify the intended inference type; evaluate an explanation using fit, scope, simplicity, and rivals; assess analogy by relevant similarities and disanalogies.  
**Common misconceptions:** Abduction does not guarantee truth, and many shared features do not help an analogy when they are irrelevant to the conclusion.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can build and compare deductive, inductive, abductive, and analogical cases for one claim. - [ ] Can state the appropriate strength of each conclusion.

## `LOG-B06` — Relevance, presumption, and common informal fallacies

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-B03`, `LOG-B04`  
**Lessons:** 1. Relevance failures—ad hominem, appeal to emotion, red herring; 2. Weak induction—hasty generalization, false cause, weak analogy; 3. Presumption—begging the question, false dilemma, loaded question; 4. Steelmanning and context-sensitive diagnosis.  
**Learning outcomes:** Diagnose why an argument fails without relying on labels alone; distinguish legitimate source criticism from abusive ad hominem; revise a fallacious argument into a stronger one.  
**Common misconceptions:** Naming a fallacy does not refute a conclusion, and rhetorical emotion is not automatically irrelevant.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can analyze ten real arguments with premise-level diagnosis. - [ ] Can improve each without changing its intended conclusion.

## `LOG-I01` — Propositional syntax and truth tables

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-B03`, `MAT-B08`  
**Lessons:** 1. Atomic propositions and connectives; 2. Scope, parentheses, translation, and well-formed formulas; 3. Truth tables and characteristic truth conditions; 4. Tautology, contradiction, contingency, equivalence, and validity.  
**Learning outcomes:** Translate controlled prose into propositional logic; construct truth tables; decide equivalence and validity semantically.  
**Common misconceptions:** Inclusive disjunction differs from everyday exclusive “or,” and the material conditional does not express every causal or relevant conditional.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can translate and truth-test a five-connective argument. - [ ] Can explain every row that invalidates an inference.

## `LOG-I02` — Natural deduction and semantic consequence

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-I01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Introduction and elimination rules; 2. Conditional and indirect proof; 3. Assumptions, subproofs, and discharge; 4. Derivability versus semantic consequence; 5. Proof strategy and countermodel choice.  
**Learning outcomes:** Construct formal derivations; explain the legitimacy of each rule application; choose proof or countermodel efficiently.  
**Common misconceptions:** A proof may use assumptions temporarily without asserting them globally, and failed proof search does not establish invalidity.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can complete derivations using all core connectives. - [ ] Can pair an underivable invalid argument with a countervaluation.

## `LOG-I03` — Predicate logic, quantifiers, identity, and relations

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-I02`, `MAT-I01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Predicates, names, variables, domains; 2. Universal and existential quantification; 3. Quantifier scope and multiple quantification; 4. Identity and definite descriptions; 5. Properties of binary relations and first-order translation.  
**Learning outcomes:** Translate quantified prose; detect scope ambiguity; construct models and countermodels for first-order claims; reason with identity and relations.  
**Common misconceptions:** “Not all” means “some not,” not “none,” and reversing nested quantifiers changes meaning.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can translate and evaluate quantified arguments over a stated domain. - [ ] Can build a finite countermodel for an invalid inference.

## `LOG-I04` — Inductive logic, confirmation, and base-rate reasoning

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-B05`, `STA-B03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Sample-to-population and population-to-case inference; 2. Prior probability, likelihood, and posterior direction; 3. Confirmation, disconfirmation, and auxiliary assumptions; 4. Base rates, diagnostic evidence, and natural frequencies; 5. Selection and stopping effects.  
**Learning outcomes:** Evaluate inductive support relative to background knowledge; solve base-rate problems; explain why the same evidence can affect hypotheses differently.  
**Common misconceptions:** A high test accuracy is not a high posterior probability, and observing predicted evidence does not uniquely confirm one hypothesis.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can solve diagnostic problems using natural frequencies. - [ ] Can list rival hypotheses and discriminating evidence.

## `LOG-I05` — Causal argument, explanation, mechanisms, and rival hypotheses

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-I04`, `RSH-B03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Correlation, temporal order, intervention, and counterfactual dependence; 2. Mechanisms and causal chains; 3. Confounding, reverse causation, selection, and common cause; 4. Necessary, sufficient, contributory, and probabilistic causes; 5. Comparative explanation and severe tests.  
**Learning outcomes:** Draw a causal structure from prose; identify rival explanations; specify evidence that would discriminate among them; avoid overstating causal strength.  
**Common misconceptions:** Temporal precedence alone does not establish causation, and “multifactorial” does not mean causal analysis is impossible.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can critique a causal media claim with a diagram and alternatives. - [ ] Can propose a feasible discriminating study.

## `LOG-I06` — Modal, deontic, temporal, and epistemic logic orientation

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-I03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Necessity, possibility, and possible-world semantics; 2. Obligation, permission, and prohibition; 3. Time, tense, and branching futures; 4. Knowledge, belief, accessibility, and common knowledge; 5. Paradoxes and applications.  
**Learning outcomes:** Formalize elementary modal, normative, temporal, and epistemic claims; distinguish necessity from actuality and obligation from truth; evaluate simple models.  
**Common misconceptions:** “Must” can express logical, physical, legal, or practical necessity, and obligation does not entail compliance.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can disambiguate and symbolize mixed modal language. - [ ] Can evaluate formulas on a small accessibility model.

## `LOG-A01` — Soundness, completeness, compactness, and Löwenheim–Skolem orientation

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-I03`, `MAT-A08`  
**Lessons:** 1. Formal languages, structures, satisfaction, and consequence; 2. Metalanguage and object language; 3. Soundness and completeness proof architecture; 4. Compactness and model construction; 5. Löwenheim–Skolem results and philosophical significance.  
**Learning outcomes:** State the major metatheorems precisely; sketch their proof strategies; derive standard consequences of compactness; explain apparent paradoxes without conflating syntax and semantics.  
**Common misconceptions:** Completeness of first-order logic is not Gödel incompleteness, and countable models do not make uncountable sets externally countable.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can prove a basic compactness application. - [ ] Can compare semantic completeness with syntactic incompleteness.

## `LOG-A02` — Computability, proof systems, paradoxes, and limits of formalization

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-I03`, `CSC-I04`, `MAT-A08`  
**Lessons:** 1. Effective procedure and equivalent computation models; 2. Encoding, diagonalization, and self-reference; 3. Halting and undecidability; 4. Gödel incompleteness; 5. Semantic, set-theoretic, and vagueness paradoxes; 6. Scope of formal limits.  
**Learning outcomes:** Prove a simple undecidability result by reduction; outline diagonal arguments; state incompleteness without popular exaggeration; classify major paradox families.  
**Common misconceptions:** Incompleteness does not show that all truth is subjective or that human minds can solve every uncomputable problem.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can construct a correct halting-style contradiction. - [ ] Can identify exactly which assumptions a formal limitation uses.

## `LOG-E01` — Model theory, proof theory, and nonclassical logics

**Prerequisites:** `LOG-A01`, `LOG-A02`  
**Lessons:** 1. Elementary equivalence, definability, types, and saturation; 2. Sequent calculi, normalization, and cut elimination; 3. Intuitionistic and many-valued semantics; 4. Relevant, paraconsistent, and substructural systems; 5. Research comparison of expressive and proof-theoretic trade-offs.  
**Learning outcomes:** Read advanced formal-logic literature; prove representative metatheoretic results; select and defend a nonclassical logic for a modeled phenomenon.  
**Common misconceptions:** Rejecting one classical principle need not reject rational inference, and no single nonclassical logic solves every semantic or inferential problem.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can present a complete metatheorem proof in one selected system. - [ ] Can compare two systems by semantics, proof theory, and application.
