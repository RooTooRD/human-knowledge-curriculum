# Logic Glossary

These are curriculum-scoped working definitions. They identify how a term is used
in its unit and the operation mastery requires; consult the unit’s advanced
reference for competing, historical, or specialist definitions.

| Unit | Terms |
|---|---|
| `LOG-B01` | **Claims:** Within Claims, questions, definitions, and arguments, the organizing beginner idea that connects declarative, interrogative, imperative, and expressive utterances with claims and truth conditions; in this curriculum it supports the ability to classify utterances by logical role.<br>**questions:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through argument versus explanation, report, illustration, and persuasion; mastery includes the ability to test definitions for scope and circularity. |
| `LOG-B02` | **Premises:** Within Premises, conclusions, inference indicators, and argument maps, the organizing beginner idea that connects explicit and implicit premises with conclusion and premise indicators; in this curriculum it supports the ability to reconstruct an argument charitably in standard form.<br>**conclusions:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through standard form and argument mapping; mastery includes the ability to supply a necessary implicit premise. |
| `LOG-B03` | **Validity:** Within Validity, soundness, strength, cogency, and counterexample, the organizing beginner idea that connects necessary versus probable support with validity and invalidating interpretations; in this curriculum it supports the ability to evaluate deductive and inductive arguments using the correct standards.<br>**soundness:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through counterexample construction; mastery includes the ability to construct a counterexample to an invalid form. |
| `LOG-B04` | **Ambiguity:** Within Ambiguity, vagueness, equivocation, and operational definition, the organizing beginner idea that connects lexical and syntactic ambiguity with borderline cases and sorites structure; in this curriculum it supports the ability to generate and disambiguate multiple readings.<br>**vagueness:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through costs of measurement proxies; mastery includes the ability to locate a meaning shift that invalidates an argument. |
| `LOG-B05` | **Deduction:** Within Deduction, induction, abduction, and analogy, the organizing beginner idea that connects deductive entailment with enumerative and statistical induction; in this curriculum it supports the ability to identify the intended inference type.<br>**induction:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through choosing an inference type; mastery includes the ability to evaluate an explanation using fit, scope, simplicity, and rivals. |
| `LOG-B06` | **Relevance:** Within Relevance, presumption, and common informal fallacies, the organizing beginner idea that connects relevance failures—ad hominem, appeal to emotion, red herring with weak induction—hasty generalization, false cause, weak analogy; in this curriculum it supports the ability to diagnose why an argument fails without relying on labels alone.<br>**presumption:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through steelmanning and context-sensitive diagnosis; mastery includes the ability to distinguish legitimate source criticism from abusive ad hominem. |
| `LOG-I01` | **Propositional syntax tables:** Within Propositional syntax and truth tables, the organizing intermediate idea that connects atomic propositions and connectives with scope, parentheses, translation, and well-formed formulas; in this curriculum it supports the ability to translate controlled prose into propositional logic.<br>**truth tables:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through tautology, contradiction, contingency, equivalence, and validity; mastery includes the ability to construct truth tables. |
| `LOG-I02` | **Natural deduction consequence:** Within Natural deduction and semantic consequence, the organizing intermediate idea that connects introduction and elimination rules with conditional and indirect proof; in this curriculum it supports the ability to construct formal derivations.<br>**semantic consequence:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through proof strategy and countermodel choice; mastery includes the ability to explain the legitimacy of each rule application. |
| `LOG-I03` | **Predicate logic:** Within Predicate logic, quantifiers, identity, and relations, the organizing intermediate idea that connects predicates, names, variables, domains with universal and existential quantification; in this curriculum it supports the ability to translate quantified prose.<br>**quantifiers:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through properties of binary relations and first-order translation; mastery includes the ability to detect scope ambiguity. |
| `LOG-A01` | **Soundness:** Within Soundness, completeness, compactness, and Löwenheim–Skolem orientation, the organizing advanced idea that connects formal languages, structures, satisfaction, and consequence with metalanguage and object language; in this curriculum it supports the ability to state the major metatheorems precisely.<br>**completeness:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through löwenheim–Skolem results and philosophical significance; mastery includes the ability to sketch their proof strategies. |
| `LOG-I04` | **Inductive logic:** Within Inductive logic, confirmation, and base-rate reasoning, the organizing intermediate idea that connects sample-to-population and population-to-case inference with prior probability, likelihood, and posterior direction; in this curriculum it supports the ability to evaluate inductive support relative to background knowledge.<br>**confirmation:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through selection and stopping effects; mastery includes the ability to solve base-rate problems. |
| `LOG-I05` | **Causal argument:** Within Causal argument, explanation, mechanisms, and rival hypotheses, the organizing intermediate idea that connects correlation, temporal order, intervention, and counterfactual dependence with mechanisms and causal chains; in this curriculum it supports the ability to draw a causal structure from prose.<br>**explanation:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through comparative explanation and severe tests; mastery includes the ability to identify rival explanations. |
| `LOG-I06` | **Modal:** Within Modal, deontic, temporal, and epistemic logic orientation, the organizing intermediate idea that connects necessity, possibility, and possible-world semantics with obligation, permission, and prohibition; in this curriculum it supports the ability to formalize elementary modal, normative, temporal, and epistemic claims.<br>**deontic:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through paradoxes and applications; mastery includes the ability to distinguish necessity from actuality and obligation from truth. |
| `LOG-A02` | **Computability:** Within Computability, proof systems, paradoxes, and limits of formalization, the organizing advanced idea that connects effective procedure and equivalent computation models with encoding, diagonalization, and self-reference; in this curriculum it supports the ability to prove a simple undecidability result by reduction.<br>**proof systems:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through scope of formal limits; mastery includes the ability to outline diagonal arguments. |
| `LOG-E01` | **Model theory:** Within Model theory, proof theory, and nonclassical logics, the organizing expert idea that connects elementary equivalence, definability, types, and saturation with sequent calculi, normalization, and cut elimination; in this curriculum it supports the ability to read advanced formal-logic literature.<br>**proof theory:** A concept, distinction, or method in this unit, developed through research comparison of expressive and proof-theoretic trade-offs; mastery includes the ability to prove representative metatheoretic results. |
