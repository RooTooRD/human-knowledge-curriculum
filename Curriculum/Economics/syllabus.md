# Economics Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ECO-B01` Scarcity, opportunity cost, incentives, margins, production | `FND-I02`, `MAT-B06` | 1. Choice/constraint; 2. opportunity cost; 3. marginal analysis; 4. incentives/unintended effects; 5. production/frontiers | Model constrained choice; calculate opportunity cost; predict incentive responses | Cost means forgone alternative, not only money spent | ☐ Analyze everyday choice; ☐ construct production frontier |
| `ECO-B02` Supply, demand, elasticity, equilibrium, surplus, adjustment | `ECO-B01`, `MAT-B05` | 1. Demand/supply; 2. shifts/movements; 3. equilibrium/disequilibrium; 4. elasticity; 5. surplus/controls/taxes | Solve market models; distinguish shifts; calculate incidence/welfare | Price changes do not shift a curve for the same good | ☐ Analyze policy graphically/algebraically; ☐ explain incidence |
| `ECO-I01` Consumer, firm, costs, competition, monopoly, strategy | `ECO-B02`, `MAT-I03` | 1. Preferences/budget; 2. production/cost; 3. competitive firm; 4. monopoly/oligopoly; 5. market structure | Derive choices/supply; compare structures; calculate welfare/markup | Profit maximization does not mean maximizing price or revenue | ☐ Solve firm/consumer problems; ☐ analyze industry |
| `ECO-I02` Game theory, information, uncertainty, contracts, behavioral economics | `ECO-I01`, `STA-B03`, `PSY-I04` | 1. Strategic games; 2. sequential/repeated; 3. adverse selection/moral hazard/signaling; 4. contracts/auctions; 5. biases/social preference | Solve strategic/information problems; design incentives; compare behavioral predictions | “Irrational” behavior may be systematic, contextual, or model-relative | ☐ Solve games/contracts; ☐ test behavioral hypothesis |
| `ECO-I03` Institutions, property, transaction costs, externalities, public goods, inequality, welfare | `ECO-B02`, `PHI-I06` | 1. Institutions/property; 2. transaction costs; 3. externality/public good/common resource; 4. distribution/inequality; 5. welfare and policy | Diagnose market/institution failure; compare remedies; expose value assumptions | Market failure does not imply any government intervention will succeed | ☐ Evaluate institutional alternatives; ☐ conduct welfare/distribution analysis |
| `ECO-B03` National accounts, growth, inflation, unemployment, money, cycles | `ECO-B01`, `STA-B04` | 1. GDP/income/expenditure; 2. price indices/inflation; 3. labor measures; 4. money/banking; 5. growth/cycles | Calculate/interpret macro indicators; distinguish nominal/real; critique GDP limits | GDP is flow production, not wealth or well-being | ☐ Audit national indicators; ☐ explain cycle data |
| `ECO-I04` Macroeconomic models, fiscal/monetary policy, expectations, stabilization | `ECO-B03`, `MAT-I07` | 1. Aggregate demand/supply; 2. consumption/investment; 3. fiscal multipliers/debt; 4. money/rates/central bank; 5. expectations/open economy | Analyze shocks/policies; compare horizons; state assumptions/trade-offs | Government budget is not mechanically equivalent to household budget | ☐ Model recession/inflation response; ☐ defend policy mix |
| `ECO-I05` Banking, credit, financial systems, crises, central banks | `ECO-B03`, `FIN-B01` | 1. Intermediation/money creation; 2. balance sheets/leverage; 3. liquidity/solvency; 4. runs/contagion/crises; 5. lender of last resort/regulation | Read bank balance sheet; model run/crisis; compare safeguards | Banks do not simply lend preexisting deposits one-for-one | ☐ Stress-test bank; ☐ analyze historical crisis mechanism |
| `ECO-I06` International trade, finance, exchange rates, migration, globalization | `ECO-B02`, `ECO-B03`, `GEO-I01` | 1. Comparative advantage; 2. trade distribution/policy; 3. balance of payments; 4. exchange regimes/capital; 5. migration/global chains | Calculate gains/distribution; analyze exchange/balance accounts; evaluate policy | Trade can raise total gains while producing concentrated losses | ☐ Solve trade/FX model; ☐ write distribution-aware policy brief |
| `ECO-I07` Growth, development, poverty, capability, institutions, structural change | `ECO-I03`, `ECO-I04`, `HST-I08` | 1. Growth accounting; 2. poverty/inequality/capability; 3. institutions/geography/history; 4. structural transformation; 5. development interventions | Compare development measures/theories; analyze constraints; evaluate interventions | Development is not GDP growth alone or one universal sequence | ☐ Diagnose country pathway; ☐ appraise development evidence |
| `ECO-I08` Labor, education, health, urban, environmental, public economics | `ECO-I03`, `STA-I04` | 1. Labor supply/demand; 2. human capital/signaling; 3. health/insurance; 4. cities/housing; 5. tax, environment, public spending | Apply economic tools across sectors; analyze incidence/incentives; recognize domain institutions | Wage differences do not directly measure individual productivity or merit | ☐ Complete sector analysis; ☐ evaluate policy with equity |
| `ECO-A01` Econometrics, identification, experiments, policy evaluation | `STA-A01`, `ECO-I01` | 1. Economic estimands; 2. regression/endogeneity; 3. experiments; 4. IV/RD/DiD/panels; 5. robustness/external validity | Identify credible designs; estimate effects; critique policy evidence | Sophisticated regression cannot manufacture identification | ☐ Replicate causal paper; ☐ defend identification |
| `ECO-A02` Political economy, collective choice, regulation, state capacity | `ECO-I03`, `POL-I03`, `SYS-A01` | 1. Voting/social choice; 2. collective action; 3. bureaucracy/state capacity; 4. regulation/capture; 5. conflict/institutions | Model political incentives; analyze reform feasibility; compare state/market capacity | Inefficient institutions may persist because they benefit powerful actors | ☐ Analyze reform coalition; ☐ design incentive-compatible institution |
| `ECO-A03` History of economic thought and comparative systems | `ECO-I07`, `HST-I08`, `PHI-I06` | 1. Ancient/Islamic/scholastic economy; 2. classical/Marxian; 3. marginalist/Keynesian; 4. institutional/Austrian/development; 5. capitalist/socialist/mixed systems | Contextualize theories; compare systems empirically/normatively; trace concept change | Past economists should not be reduced to modern slogans | ☐ Analyze primary text; ☐ compare systems across institutions/outcomes |
| `ECO-A04` Climate, energy, resources, innovation, transition | `ECO-I08`, `ECS-I05`, `SYS-A04` | 1. Resource scarcity/rents; 2. pollution/carbon pricing; 3. innovation/learning/spillovers; 4. transition distribution; 5. uncertainty/irreversibility | Value externalities; compare instruments; model innovation/transition | One carbon price may not address every infrastructure/innovation barrier | ☐ Build transition policy model; ☐ stress-test equity/uncertainty |
| `ECO-A05` Advanced microeconomic and macroeconomic theory | `ECO-I02`, `ECO-I04`, `MAT-A05` | 1. General equilibrium/welfare; 2. information/mechanisms; 3. dynamic optimization; 4. growth/RBC/New Keynesian models; 5. heterogeneity/computation | Derive equilibria; solve dynamic models; evaluate empirical relevance | Internal model consistency does not guarantee external validity | ☐ Complete advanced derivations; ☐ calibrate/critique model |
| `ECO-E01` Economic research and model criticism | `ECO-A01`, `ECO-A05`, `RSH-E01` | 1. Question/contribution; 2. theory/institution; 3. data/identification; 4. robustness/welfare; 5. reproducibility/policy translation | Produce original economic research; connect model and institution; make calibrated policy inference | Causal estimate alone does not supply welfare judgment or general equilibrium effects | ☐ Deliver original paper/code; ☐ pass seminar defense |
