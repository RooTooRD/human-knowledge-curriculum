# Foundations Syllabus

## `FND-B01` — Language, reference, and ordinary meaning

**Prerequisites:** none  
**Lessons:** 1. Signs and referents—objects, actions, properties, names; 2. Words in context—literal meaning, convention, deixis, polysemy; 3. Utterances—speaker, listener, intent, shared context, misunderstanding.  
**Learning outcomes:** Identify what common words and sentences refer to; restate an utterance while preserving its ordinary meaning; detect when context changes meaning.  
**Common misconceptions:** A word is not naturally or permanently attached to its referent, and dictionary entries do not eliminate contextual judgment.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can identify referent, speaker, audience, and context in ten ordinary utterances. - [ ] Can resolve a simple ambiguity by asking a precise question.

## `FND-B02` — Counting, quantity, comparison, and estimation

**Prerequisites:** none  
**Lessons:** 1. One-to-one counting and cardinality; 2. More, less, equal, ordering, and zero; 3. Grouping and place-value intuition; 4. Exact count versus estimate, bounds, and reasonableness.  
**Learning outcomes:** Count finite collections accurately; compare quantities; produce and justify a useful order-of-magnitude estimate.  
**Common misconceptions:** The last number word gives the collection's cardinality, not merely the name of its final object; an estimate is constrained rather than arbitrary.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can count and compare unfamiliar collections without double-counting. - [ ] Can estimate a visible quantity and state a defensible interval.

## `FND-B03` — Observation, description, classification, and pattern

**Prerequisites:** none  
**Lessons:** 1. Observation versus inference; 2. Descriptive attributes and measurement; 3. Classification by explicit criteria; 4. Similarity, difference, sequence, repetition, and anomaly.  
**Learning outcomes:** Produce descriptions that separate observation from interpretation; construct and revise a classification rule; identify a pattern and a counterexample.  
**Common misconceptions:** Observations are selective and concept-laden, and a perceived pattern does not by itself establish a cause.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can label each sentence in a sample as observation or inference. - [ ] Can classify a mixed set and explain every disputed case.

## `FND-B04` — Agency, goals, habits, and responsibility

**Prerequisites:** none  
**Lessons:** 1. Needs, wants, values, and goals; 2. Choice, constraint, consequence, and responsibility; 3. Cues, routines, rewards, and habit loops; 4. Planning, action, reflection, and revision.  
**Learning outcomes:** Convert an aspiration into a specific controllable goal; distinguish controllable action from external constraint; design and review a simple habit.  
**Common misconceptions:** Agency is neither unlimited control nor helplessness; motivation alone is less reliable than an environment and routine designed for action.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can write a goal with action, trigger, measure, and review date. - [ ] Can diagnose why a habit failed without confusing explanation with excuse.

## `FND-B05` — Reading words, sentences, paragraphs, and instructions

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Decoding and fluent phrasing; 2. Sentence roles, connectors, pronouns, and negation; 3. Topic, main claim, support, and paragraph sequence; 4. Procedural language, conditions, warnings, and verification.  
**Learning outcomes:** Read ordinary prose accurately; identify a paragraph's main point and support; execute and check multistep written instructions.  
**Common misconceptions:** Reading aloud fluently does not guarantee comprehension, and the first sentence is not always the main claim.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can summarize a short passage without adding unsupported content. - [ ] Can follow a new procedure and identify its decision points.

## `FND-B06` — Vocabulary building and dictionary use

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B01`, `FND-B05`  
**Lessons:** 1. Context clues and word families; 2. Denotation, connotation, register, and collocation; 3. Dictionary headwords, senses, labels, pronunciation, and examples; 4. Personal vocabulary capture, retrieval, and use.  
**Learning outcomes:** Infer a provisional meaning from context; select the relevant dictionary sense; use a new word accurately in multiple contexts.  
**Common misconceptions:** A word does not have one interchangeable synonym, and memorizing a definition without usage does not produce command.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can disambiguate five polysemous words using context and a dictionary. - [ ] Can correctly reuse ten newly learned words after a delay.

## `FND-B07` — Time, scale, measurement, and spatial orientation

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B02`, `FND-B03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Before/after, duration, calendars, clocks, and timelines; 2. Length, mass, volume, temperature, and unit comparison; 3. Position, direction, distance, route, and coordinate intuition; 4. Human, microscopic, geographic, planetary, and historical scales.  
**Learning outcomes:** Read common measures and schedules; convert within familiar unit systems; orient a route or object using relational and coordinate descriptions.  
**Common misconceptions:** A map's visual size is not physical size, and equal numerical changes can represent very different proportional changes.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can plan a timed route from a map and schedule. - [ ] Can choose an appropriate unit and estimate before measuring.

## `FND-B08` — Basic digital-device and file operation

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B04`, `FND-B05`  
**Lessons:** 1. Hardware, operating system, application, account, and network; 2. Input, navigation, settings, accessibility, and updates; 3. Files, folders, names, extensions, search, copy, move, and deletion; 4. Local/cloud storage, synchronization, backup, and safe shutdown.  
**Learning outcomes:** Operate a common device without losing work; organize and retrieve files; distinguish device, application, account, and network failures.  
**Common misconceptions:** Cloud files still reside on physical systems, synchronization is not the same as backup, and deleting a shortcut need not delete its target.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can create, rename, move, find, export, and back up a document. - [ ] Can diagnose a basic connection or account problem systematically.

## `FND-B09` — Reading tables, diagrams, maps, and interfaces

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B05`, `FND-B07`  
**Lessons:** 1. Rows, columns, headers, units, totals, and missing values; 2. Legends, axes, arrows, nodes, layers, and scale; 3. Map symbols, orientation, projection awareness, and route; 4. Interface hierarchy, state, controls, feedback, and error messages.  
**Learning outcomes:** Extract a precise value or relationship from a visual representation; translate between prose and a simple diagram; navigate an unfamiliar interface by interpreting feedback.  
**Common misconceptions:** Visual proximity does not always mean conceptual relation, and polished graphics are not necessarily accurate.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can answer comparison questions from a table, map, and process diagram. - [ ] Can explain what an interface currently did and how that state is signaled.

## `FND-B10` — Questions, uncertainty, and checking

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B01`, `FND-B03`  
**Lessons:** 1. Open, closed, clarifying, causal, evaluative, and procedural questions; 2. Known, unknown, uncertain, and unknowable; 3. Predictions and checks; 4. Cross-checking, reverse operations, independent confirmation, and error logs.  
**Learning outcomes:** Turn confusion into a precise question; express the source and degree of uncertainty; select a check capable of exposing an error.  
**Common misconceptions:** Confidence is not evidence, and repeating the same method is not an independent check.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can rewrite five vague questions into answerable ones. - [ ] Can design two independent checks for an everyday claim or calculation.

## `FND-I01` — Information navigation and source orientation

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B05`, `FND-B08`  
**Lessons:** 1. Information need and search vocabulary; 2. Contents, index, abstract, headings, metadata, and hyperlinks; 3. Search operators, filters, result snippets, and query revision; 4. Author, publisher, date, genre, purpose, and citation trail.  
**Learning outcomes:** Locate a specific fact and an explanatory source efficiently; revise a failed query; record enough source metadata to return and cite it.  
**Common misconceptions:** Search ranking is not a quality ranking, and a result snippet is not evidence that the linked source supports a claim.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can find two independent source types for one question and document the search path. - [ ] Can identify author, venue, date, and purpose before relying on a source.

## `FND-I02` — Everyday quantitative judgment

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B02`, `FND-B07`  
**Lessons:** 1. Rates, ratios, percentages, and percentage points; 2. Averages, ranges, totals, and denominators; 3. Growth, compounding intuition, and orders of magnitude; 4. Unit prices, capacity, time, risk, and sanity checks.  
**Learning outcomes:** Compare quantities on a common basis; choose the relevant denominator; estimate the practical effect of a rate or percentage.  
**Common misconceptions:** Percent and percentage-point changes differ, averages need not describe a typical case, and relative change without a baseline can mislead.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can audit a news or shopping claim involving percentages. - [ ] Can estimate a total from a rate and expose an impossible answer by units.

## `FND-I03` — Fact, model, interpretation, opinion, and value

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B05`, `FND-B10`  
**Lessons:** 1. Observable claims and records; 2. Models, assumptions, idealization, and scope; 3. Interpretation, perspective, and underdetermination; 4. Preferences, evaluations, norms, and mixed claims.  
**Learning outcomes:** Classify components of a complex statement; state what evidence could bear on each component; distinguish disagreement about facts from disagreement about meaning or value.  
**Common misconceptions:** Interpretations are not therefore arbitrary, models are not intended as literal copies, and value-laden questions can still be reasoned about.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can decompose a public claim into factual, modeled, interpretive, and normative parts. - [ ] Can name an appropriate warrant for each part.

## `FND-I04` — Personal safety, consent, boundaries, and help-seeking

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B04`, `FND-B05`  
**Lessons:** 1. Hazard, warning sign, immediate danger, and precaution; 2. Consent as informed, voluntary, specific, ongoing, and revocable; 3. Physical, emotional, sexual, financial, and digital boundaries; 4. Trusted people, emergency services, documentation, reporting, and bystander action.  
**Learning outcomes:** Recognize situations requiring exit or help; communicate and respect a boundary; choose an appropriate escalation path while preserving evidence and safety.  
**Common misconceptions:** Silence or prior agreement is not ongoing consent, and seeking help is not failure or betrayal.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can respond correctly to three safety scenarios with different urgency. - [ ] Can state and receive a boundary without bargaining past refusal.

## `FND-I05` — Civic and institutional orientation

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B04`, `FND-I01`  
**Lessons:** 1. Household, school, workplace, association, market, court, municipality, and state; 2. Roles, rules, records, offices, jurisdictions, and escalation; 3. Public/private, right/service, tax/fee, complaint/appeal; 4. Identification, contracts, licenses, voting, and public information.  
**Learning outcomes:** Identify which institution has authority over an ordinary problem; navigate a basic administrative process; distinguish a right, obligation, service, and discretionary benefit.  
**Common misconceptions:** “The government” and “the company” are not single actors, and the first official contacted may lack jurisdiction.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can map an everyday dispute to the responsible office and appeal path. - [ ] Can assemble the records required for a routine application.

## `FND-I06` — Integrated foundational literacy

**Prerequisites:** `FND-B06`, `FND-B09`, `FND-I02`, `FND-I03`, `FND-I04`, `FND-I05`  
**Lessons:** 1. Read a mixed prose/numeric/visual brief; 2. Identify claims, uncertainty, risk, and responsible institution; 3. Search, compare, calculate, and document; 4. Decide, communicate, act, and review.  
**Learning outcomes:** Complete an unfamiliar everyday knowledge task from question to checked action; integrate language, quantity, visuals, safety, and institutional reasoning.  
**Common misconceptions:** Foundational literacy is not a one-time threshold; unfamiliar contexts require returning to questions, sources, and checks.  
**Mastery:** - [ ] Can solve a realistic multiformat task and leave an auditable record. - [ ] Can explain which foundational tool prevented the most consequential error.
