# Physics Syllabus

| Unit | Prerequisites | Ordered lessons | Observable outcomes | Common misconception | Mastery checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PHY-B01` Physical quantities, units, estimation, and measurement | `FND-B07`, `MAT-B03`, `RSH-B03` | 1. SI quantities and vectors/scalars; 2. dimensions and conversion; 3. precision, accuracy, uncertainty; 4. order-of-magnitude estimation; 5. experimental graphing | Convert/check dimensions; propagate basic uncertainty; estimate physical scales | More decimal places do not imply greater accuracy | ☐ Audit dimensions/units; ☐ measure and report with uncertainty |
| `PHY-B02` Kinematics, vectors, frames, and motion graphs | `PHY-B01`, `MAT-B05` | 1. Position/displacement; 2. velocity/acceleration; 3. graphs and derivatives; 4. 2D/projectile motion; 5. relative frames | Translate among motion representations; solve constant-acceleration and relative-motion problems | Negative velocity is direction, not slowing | ☐ Analyze unfamiliar motion graph; ☐ solve/verify vector trajectory |
| `PHY-B03` Forces, Newtonian mechanics, momentum, energy, and rotation | `PHY-B02`, `MAT-I02` | 1. Newton laws/free bodies; 2. friction, gravity, circular motion; 3. work/energy/power; 4. impulse/momentum/collisions; 5. torque/angular momentum | Build force models; apply conservation laws; solve rotational dynamics | Motion does not require a continuing net force | ☐ Complete mixed mechanics exam; ☐ validate model experimentally |
| `PHY-B04` Charge, current, circuits, voltage, power, and magnetism | `PHY-B01`, `MAT-B05` | 1. Charge and electric force/field; 2. potential and energy; 3. current, resistance, DC circuits; 4. power/capacitance; 5. magnetic force/field | Analyze fields and circuits; measure voltage/current safely; calculate power | Current is not consumed by components, and voltage is a difference | ☐ Build/predict a circuit; ☐ map field/potential relationships |
| `PHY-B05` Oscillations, waves, sound, light, and optics | `PHY-B02`, `MAT-B06` | 1. Harmonic motion; 2. wave speed/frequency/amplitude; 3. superposition/resonance; 4. sound/Doppler; 5. reflection, refraction, lenses | Model oscillations/waves; predict interference/resonance; construct ray diagrams | Wave speed is medium-dependent, not set by amplitude | ☐ Analyze a wave system; ☐ measure optics/acoustics behavior |
| `PHY-B06` Temperature, heat, gases, entropy, and processes | `PHY-B01`, `MAT-B06` | 1. Temperature/equilibrium; 2. heat/capacity/phase; 3. ideal gases and kinetic picture; 4. first law and processes; 5. second law/entropy/engines | Apply energy balances; interpret state/process variables; calculate engine limits | Temperature is not total thermal energy, and entropy is not simply disorder | ☐ Solve thermodynamic cycles; ☐ explain irreversible process |
| `PHY-I01` Analytical and computational classical mechanics | `PHY-B03`, `MAT-I04`, `MAT-I07` | 1. Newtonian systems; 2. constraints/generalized coordinates; 3. Lagrangian action; 4. central force/rigid body; 5. numerical/chaotic dynamics | Derive equations with Newton/Lagrange methods; analyze conservation/stability; simulate trajectories | Lagrangian formalism is equivalent structure, not a new physical force | ☐ Derive and simulate multi-body system; ☐ identify symmetry/conservation |
| `PHY-I02` Electromagnetic fields and Maxwell equations | `PHY-B04`, `MAT-I05`, `MAT-I07` | 1. Electrostatics/Gauss; 2. potential/boundaries; 3. currents/magnetostatics; 4. induction; 5. Maxwell waves/energy | Solve symmetric field problems; derive EM waves; apply boundary conditions | Fields are physical model quantities, not merely forces at a distance | ☐ Derive Maxwell wave equation; ☐ solve boundary-value field problem |
| `PHY-I03` Wave equation, interference, diffraction, Fourier methods, optics | `PHY-B05`, `MAT-I07` | 1. Wave equation/modes; 2. superposition/coherence; 3. Fourier decomposition; 4. diffraction/interferometry; 5. polarization/dispersion | Solve modes; analyze spectra/interference; connect aperture and diffraction | Rays are an approximation that fails at wavelength-scale features | ☐ Analyze Fourier/wave problem; ☐ perform interference experiment |
| `PHY-I04` Thermodynamics, kinetic theory, statistical mechanics, information | `PHY-B06`, `STA-I01`, `MAT-I04` | 1. Thermodynamic potentials/relations; 2. ensembles/microstates; 3. partition function; 4. quantum/classical gases; 5. entropy/information/fluctuation | Derive macroscopic behavior statistically; select potentials; calculate distributions | Entropy increase is probabilistic/macroscopic, not violation of reversible microdynamics | ☐ Derive ideal-system properties; ☐ connect partition function to observables |
| `PHY-I05` Special relativity | `PHY-I01`, `MAT-I06` | 1. Postulates and events; 2. Lorentz transformations; 3. time/length/simultaneity; 4. four-vectors; 5. relativistic momentum/energy | Transform events and four-vectors; resolve apparent paradoxes; use invariant intervals | Relativistic effects are not optical illusions | ☐ Solve frame transformations; ☐ explain twin/ladder scenarios |
| `PHY-I06` Quantum phenomena, states, amplitudes, operators, measurement | `PHY-I03`, `MAT-I06`, `STA-I01` | 1. Quantization/interference evidence; 2. complex states and amplitudes; 3. operators/eigenvalues; 4. Born rule/measurement; 5. uncertainty/entanglement orientation | Calculate simple quantum probabilities; distinguish state/observable; explain experimental evidence | Uncertainty is not merely poor instruments, and superposition is not classical ignorance | ☐ Solve spin/two-path problems; ☐ interpret without classical category errors |
| `PHY-A01` Quantum mechanics | `PHY-I06`, `MAT-I06`, `MAT-I07` | 1. Schrödinger dynamics; 2. wells/barriers/oscillator; 3. angular momentum/spin; 4. hydrogen/atoms; 5. approximation and identical particles | Solve canonical systems; use operator/approximation methods; predict spectra | Wavefunction is not an ordinary material wave in 3D space for many particles | ☐ Derive canonical solutions; ☐ apply perturbation/variational method |
| `PHY-I07` Experimental physics | `PHY-B01`, `STA-I03`, `RSH-I04` | 1. Instrument response/calibration; 2. uncertainty/noise; 3. experimental design/control; 4. signal acquisition/fitting; 5. lab record/report/safety | Design/calibrate experiments; analyze uncertainty; distinguish systematic/random error | Agreement within error does not prove a theory uniquely | ☐ Complete independent experiment; ☐ defend uncertainty budget |
| `PHY-I08` Computational physics and numerical modeling | `PHY-I01`, `MAT-A04`, `CSC-B04` | 1. Model/discretization; 2. ODE/integration; 3. linear/eigen methods; 4. Monte Carlo; 5. verification/validation/performance | Implement numerical models; test convergence; separate numerical/model error | Smooth plots can hide unstable or unconverged computation | ☐ Reproduce analytic limiting case; ☐ deliver verified simulation |
| `PHY-A02` Condensed matter and materials | `PHY-A01`, `PHY-I04` | 1. Crystal/bonding; 2. lattice vibrations; 3. electrons/bands; 4. semiconductors/magnetism; 5. superconductivity/material properties | Explain properties from microstructure/bands; calculate simple solid models; interpret devices | Bands are not literal spatial tracks for electrons | ☐ Solve lattice/band problems; ☐ explain a material/device |
| `PHY-A03` Nuclear and particle physics | `PHY-A01`, `PHY-I05` | 1. Nuclei/binding/decay; 2. reactions/fission/fusion; 3. particles/interactions; 4. symmetries/conservation; 5. Standard Model experiments | Calculate decay/reaction kinematics; classify particles/interactions; read collision evidence | Particle diagrams depict amplitudes/terms, not photographed paths | ☐ Analyze decay/collision event; ☐ explain Standard Model scope |
| `PHY-A04` General relativity and curved spacetime | `PHY-I05`, `MAT-A07` | 1. Equivalence/geodesics; 2. metric/curvature; 3. Einstein equation; 4. Schwarzschild/orbits/black holes; 5. waves/cosmology tests | Calculate simple spacetime effects; connect geometry to matter; evaluate evidence | Gravity in GR is not a Newtonian force propagating through fixed space | ☐ Derive geodesic/redshift result; ☐ explain one empirical test |
| `PHY-A05` Fluid, plasma, continuum, and nonlinear physics | `PHY-I01`, `MAT-A10`, `SYS-I04` | 1. Continuum/conservation; 2. ideal/viscous flow; 3. instabilities/turbulence; 4. plasma/MHD; 5. nonlinear waves/patterns | Derive continuum equations; analyze dimensionless regimes/instability; model flow/plasma | Turbulence is deterministic but not therefore easily predictable | ☐ Solve continuum scaling problem; ☐ simulate/analyze instability |
| `PHY-A06` History, interpretation, and philosophy of physical theory | `PHY-I06`, `HST-I07`, `PHI-I03` | 1. Mechanics/revolution narratives; 2. field/thermodynamics; 3. relativity/quantum development; 4. realism, measurement, interpretation; 5. theory change/instrument/community | Reconstruct theories in context; compare interpretations; distinguish evidence from retrospective myth | Science history is not a linear march of lone geniuses | ☐ Write source-based episode analysis; ☐ defend an interpretation without overstating |
| `PHY-E01` Quantum field theory and frontier physics | `PHY-A01`, `PHY-A03`, `MAT-A10` | 1. Classical fields/symmetry; 2. quantization/Fock space; 3. interactions/diagrams; 4. renormalization/gauge theory; 5. frontier questions/evidence | Calculate elementary QFT processes; explain renormalization/gauge structure; evaluate frontier claims | Virtual particles are calculational features, not directly observed temporary objects | ☐ Complete graduate QFT calculations; ☐ present frontier literature synthesis |
